4.19. §4.19 Maclaurin Series and Laurent Series🔗

Source: NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §4.19.

Open this chapter in the DLMF ↗

The trigonometric functions have entire Maclaurin series for sine and cosine, while tangent and secant have their nearest poles at ±π/2. Cosecant and cotangent are represented by Laurent series with their principal parts at the origin. The final three displays are power series for the principal complex logarithm. In every entry, HasSum records both the coefficient sequence and its convergence to the stated function value. The Bernoulli coefficients use Mathlib's canonical bernoulli sequence (with B₀=1 and B₁=-1/2). The Euler coefficients use the transparent DLMF recurrence: E₀=1, odd Euler numbers vanish, and each positive even Eₙ is the negative binomial sum of the preceding even values.

DLMF 4.19.1 ↗

\sin z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n z^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}.

This series converges for every complex z, so sine is entire.

Sine Maclaurin series

DLMF 4.19.2 ↗

\cos z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n z^{2n}}{(2n)!}.

The cosine series is also entire and converges for every complex z.

Cosine Maclaurin series

DLMF 4.19.3 ↗

\tan z=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}2^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)B_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n-1}, \qquad |z|<\frac{\pi}{2}.

The tangent series is a power series about zero with radius π/2; its coefficient is written using the Bernoulli numbers B₂ₙ.

Tangent power series

DLMF 4.19.4 ↗

\csc z=\frac1z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}2(2^{2n-1}-1)B_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n-1}, \qquad 0<|z|<\pi.

This is a Laurent series on the punctured disk 0 < |z| < π; the explicit 1/z is its principal part at the origin.

Cosecant Laurent series

DLMF 4.19.5 ↗

\sec z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^nE_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n}, \qquad |z|<\frac{\pi}{2}.

The Euler numbers E₂ₙ give the even-power secant series, convergent in the disk bounded by the nearest poles.

Secant power series

DLMF 4.19.6 ↗

\cot z=\frac1z-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}2^{2n}B_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n-1}, \qquad 0<|z|<\pi.

As with cosecant, the cotangent expansion is Laurent rather than Maclaurin: the 1/z term is separated from the convergent series on the punctured disk.

DLMF 4.19.7 ↗

\ln\left(\frac{\sin z}{z}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n2^{2n-1}B_{2n}}{n(2n)!}z^{2n}, \qquad |z|<\pi.

Here ln denotes the principal complex logarithm. The disk |z|<π is the convergence domain of the even-power expansion. The Lean sinRatio name extends sin z/z at the removable center by setting sinRatio 0 = 1, so the left side is explicitly log 1 = 0 at z=0.

\ln(\cos z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n2^{2n-1}(2^{2n}-1)B_{2n}}{n(2n)!}z^{2n}, \qquad |z|<\frac{\pi}{2}.

The principal-logarithm series for cosine stops at the nearest zero of cosine, giving the radius π/2.

DLMF 4.19.9 ↗

\ln\left(\frac{\tan z}{z}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}2^{2n}(2^{2n-1}-1)B_{2n}}{n(2n)!}z^{2n}, \qquad |z|<\frac{\pi}{2}.

The final expansion is again for the principal logarithm, now of the even analytic quotient tan z / z; its disk ends at |z|=π/2. The Lean tanRatio definition makes the removable center explicit with tanRatio 0 = 1, so the logarithm at zero is again 0.
