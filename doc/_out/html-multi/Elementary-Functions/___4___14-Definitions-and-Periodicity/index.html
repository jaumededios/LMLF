4.14. §4.14 Definitions and Periodicity🔗

Source: NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §4.14.

Open this chapter in the DLMF ↗

The complex trigonometric functions are defined from the exponential, with quotient functions interpreted away from their denominator zeros. Sine and cosine are entire; their zeros are integral and half-integral multiples of π, respectively. Consequently tangent, cosecant, secant, and cotangent are meromorphic, with poles inherited from the corresponding denominator zeros.

\sin z=\frac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2i}. The sine function is defined for complex z by the displayed exponential quotient.

\tan z=\frac{\sin z}{\cos z}. Tangent is the quotient on the locus where cosine is nonzero.

Complex tangent definition

DLMF 4.14.5 ↗

\csc z=\frac1{\sin z}. Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine away from sine's zeros.

\sec z=\frac1{\cos z}. Secant is the reciprocal of cosine away from cosine's zeros.

DLMF 4.14.7 ↗

\cot z=\frac{\cos z}{\sin z}=\frac1{\tan z}. On the common domain of the quotients, cotangent has both displayed forms.

Cotangent reciprocal identity

The zeros of sine are kπ, and the zeros of cosine are (k+1/2)π, for integers k. The quotient functions are therefore meromorphic; their poles occur at the zeros of the denominator indicated in (4.14.4)–(4.14.7).

Sine and cosine zero sets

DLMF 4.14.8 ↗

\sin(z+2k\pi)=\sin z,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z. Sine has period 2π over the complex plane.

Sine periodicity

DLMF 4.14.9 ↗

\cos(z+2k\pi)=\cos z,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z. Cosine has the same period 2π.

Cosine periodicity

DLMF 4.14.10 ↗

\tan(z+k\pi)=\tan z,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z. Tangent has period π wherever the shifted point is not a pole.
