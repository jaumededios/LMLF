§4.3 Graphics🔗

The four figures in this section visualize the real inverse relationship between the logarithm and exponential, the conformal mapping of the principal strip, and the magnitude and phase of the principal logarithm and exponential. The displays below retain the source captions and make their mathematical content explicit in Lean.

§4.3(i) Real Arguments🔗

Source: DLMF §4.3(i).

DLMF 4.3.F1 ↗

\ln x\quad\text{and}\quad {\mathrm e}^{x}.

The curves are mirror images in the line y=x: their parallel tangents at (1,0) and (0,1) make the inverse relationship visible. On the real line, log and exp are inverse everywhere in the direction log (exp x) = x, and the reverse direction holds on the positive half-line.

§4.3(ii) Complex Arguments: Conformal Maps🔗

Source: DLMF §4.3(ii).

DLMF 4.3.F2 ↗

w={\mathrm e}^{z},\qquad z=\ln w.

The strip -π < Im z < π maps under w=exp z onto the w-plane cut along the negative real axis; log w is the principal value on that slit plane. Horizontal lines map to rays and vertical lines to circles centered at the origin. For a positive real parameter r, the table in the source records 0 ↦ 1, r ↦ eʳ, r+iπ ↦ -eʳ+i0, r−iπ ↦ -eʳ−i0, iπ ↦ -1+i0, −iπ ↦ -1−i0, -r+iπ ↦ -e⁻ʳ+i0, -r−iπ ↦ -e⁻ʳ−i0, and -r ↦ e⁻ʳ.

§4.3(iii) Complex Arguments: Surfaces🔗

Source: DLMF §4.3(iii).

In these graphics, height is the absolute value of the function and color is its phase. The principal logarithm has a branch cut on the negative real axis.

DLMF 4.3.F3 ↗

\left|\ln(x+\mathrm{i}y)\right|.

The surface height is the modulus of the principal logarithm at x+i y; its color records the principal phase. The displayed domain is the plane slit along the nonpositive real axis, which makes the branch-cut discontinuity visible.

Principal-logarithm surface height

DLMF 4.3.F4 ↗

\left|{\mathrm e}^{x+\mathrm{i}y}\right|.

For the exponential surface, the modulus depends only on the real coordinate: |exp(x+i y)| = exp x; the imaginary coordinate controls the phase and hence the color bands.
