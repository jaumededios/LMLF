§4.36 Infinite Products and Partial Fractions🔗

All variables are complex. E1 and E2 are interpreted as limits of the natural finite products, with factors ordered by increasing positive integer index. E3–E5 are likewise limits of finite partial-fraction sums; the pole sets are stated explicitly, so no divergent totalized expression is identified with a meromorphic value. The source notes that these formulas follow from the trigonometric products and partial fractions of §4.22 after replacing z by i z.

DLMF 4.36.1 ↗

\sinh z=z\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z^2}{n^2\pi^2}\right)$$

The finite approximant sinhProduct z N retains the source's order and the limit statement records convergence for every complex z.

DLMF 4.36.2 ↗

\cosh z=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{4z^2}{(2n-1)^2\pi^2}\right)$$

The odd positive integers index the factors, and coshProduct makes that ordering explicit before taking the limit.

DLMF 4.36.3 ↗

\coth z=\frac1z+2z\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{z^2+n^2\pi^2},\quad z\ne n\pi i.$$

The displayed partial fractions converge away from every zero of sinh; the finite approximant uses the positive-index order and the Lean target is the explicit quotient cosh z / sinh z.

DLMF 4.36.4 ↗

\operatorname{csch}^2z=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac1{(z-n\pi i)^2}$$

The symmetric finite sum cschSqPartial z N ranges from -N through N. This ordering is part of the convergence convention, and all lattice poles are excluded.

DLMF 4.36.5 ↗

\operatorname{csch} z=\frac1z+2z\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{z^2+n^2\pi^2}$$

The alternating positive-index partial fractions converge on the same pole-free domain as E3. The finite cschPartial approximants avoid assigning a value to the singular lattice.
