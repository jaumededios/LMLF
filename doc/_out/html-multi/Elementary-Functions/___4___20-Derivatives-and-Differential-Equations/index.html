§4.20 Derivatives and Differential Equations🔗

The derivative statements below use the complex variable z. The quotients secant, cosecant, and cotangent are defined by their displayed complex quotients, so their derivative formulas explicitly exclude the corresponding zeros of cosine or sine.

Complex trigonometric vocabulary
def sec (z : ℂ) : ℂ := 1 / Complex.cos zdef csc (z : ℂ) : ℂ := 1 / Complex.sin zdef cot (z : ℂ) : ℂ := Complex.cos z / Complex.sin z
DLMF 4.20.1 ↗

\frac{d}{dz}\sin z=\cos z.

Sine differentiates to cosine throughout the complex plane.

Derivative of sine
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_1 (z : ℂ) : deriv Complex.sin z = Complex.cos z
DLMF 4.20.2 ↗

\frac{d}{dz}\cos z=-\sin z.

Cosine differentiates to the negative of sine.

Derivative of cosine
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_2 (z : ℂ) : deriv Complex.cos z = -Complex.sin z
DLMF 4.20.3 ↗

\frac{d}{dz}\tan z=\sec ^2z.

At points where cos z is nonzero, tangent has derivative sec z squared.

Derivative of tangent away from poles
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_3 {z : ℂ} (hz : Complex.cos z ≠ 0) : deriv Complex.tan z = sec z ^ 2
DLMF 4.20.4 ↗

\frac{d}{dz}\csc z=-\csc z\cot z.

The cosecant derivative is stated away from the zeros of sine.

Derivative of cosecant away from poles
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_4 {z : ℂ} (hz : Complex.sin z ≠ 0) : deriv csc z = -csc z * cot z
DLMF 4.20.5 ↗

\frac{d}{dz}\sec z=\sec z\tan z.

The secant derivative is valid away from the zeros of cosine.

Derivative of secant away from poles
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_5 {z : ℂ} (hz : Complex.cos z ≠ 0) : deriv sec z = sec z * Complex.tan z
DLMF 4.20.6 ↗

\frac{d}{dz}\cot z=-\csc ^2z.

Cotangent differentiates to negative cosecant squared wherever sine is nonzero.

Derivative of cotangent away from poles
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_6 {z : ℂ} (hz : Complex.sin z ≠ 0) : deriv cot z = -(csc z) ^ 2
DLMF 4.20.7 ↗

\frac{d^n}{dz^n}\sin z=\sin\left(z+\frac12n\pi\right).

For every natural number n, the nth derivative is a phase-shifted sine. The Lean statement uses iteratedDeriv and the complex cast of n.

All derivatives of sine
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_7 (n : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : iteratedDeriv n Complex.sin z = Complex.sin (z + (n : ℂ) * (Real.pi / 2))
DLMF 4.20.8 ↗

\frac{d^n}{dz^n}\cos z=\cos\left(z+\frac12n\pi\right).

The corresponding formula for every derivative of cosine has the same phase shift.

All derivatives of cosine
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_8 (n : ℕ) (z : ℂ) : iteratedDeriv n Complex.cos z = Complex.cos (z + (n : ℂ) * (Real.pi / 2))

For the differential equations, a is a nonzero complex constant. The following explicit candidates make the solution-family distinction visible; the differential equations are asserted for these candidates rather than for an unconstrained arbitrary function.

Differential-equation candidates
def secondOrderCandidate (a A B z : ℂ) : ℂ := A * Complex.cos (a * z) + B * Complex.sin (a * z)def energyCandidate (a c z : ℂ) : ℂ := (1 / a) * Complex.sin (a * z + c)def riccatiCandidate (a c z : ℂ) : ℂ := (1 / a) * Complex.tan (a * z + c)
DLMF 4.20.9 ↗

\frac{d^2w}{dz^2}+a^2w=0.

The displayed equation is evaluated on the explicit sine-cosine candidate family. The source's standing parameter condition is a ≠ 0, even though the differential identity itself also makes sense at a = 0.

Second-order trigonometric equation
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_9 (a A B z : ℂ) (ha : a ≠ 0) : deriv (fun z => deriv (fun z => secondOrderCandidate a A B z) z) z + a ^ 2 * secondOrderCandidate a A B z = 0
DLMF 4.20.10 ↗

\left(\frac{dw}{dz}\right)^2+a^2w^2=1.

The first-order energy equation is evaluated on its phase-shifted sine candidate, under the same standing condition a ≠ 0.

First-order energy equation
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_10 (a c z : ℂ) (ha : a ≠ 0) : (deriv (fun z => energyCandidate a c z) z) ^ 2 + a ^ 2 * (energyCandidate a c z) ^ 2 = 1
DLMF 4.20.11 ↗

\frac{dw}{dz}-a^2w^2=1.

The Riccati equation is evaluated on the tangent family at points where cos(az+c) is nonzero; the standing parameter condition is again a ≠ 0.

Riccati equation
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_11 (a c z : ℂ) (ha : a ≠ 0) (hcos : Complex.cos (a * z + c) ≠ 0) : deriv (fun z => riccatiCandidate a c z) z - a ^ 2 * (riccatiCandidate a c z) ^ 2 = 1

The general solution families of these three equations are given next. The Lean equivalences characterize every globally differentiable solution in the first two cases. The Riccati family is local on an explicitly open, preconnected domain, because tangent solutions have poles. In the first family A and B are arbitrary complex constants; in the latter two c is arbitrary.

DLMF 4.20.12 ↗

w=A\cos(az)+B\sin(az).

Every globally differentiable solution is represented by a choice of the displayed sine-cosine family parameters; conversely every such choice solves the equation.

General second-order solution family
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_12 (a : ℂ) (ha : a ≠ 0) : ∀ w : ℂ → ℂ, (Differentiable ℂ w ∧ (∀ z, deriv (fun z => deriv w z) z + a ^ 2 * w z = 0)) ↔ ∃ A B : ℂ, ∀ z, w z = secondOrderCandidate a A B z
DLMF 4.20.13 ↗

w=\frac1a\sin(az+c).

Every globally differentiable nonconstant solution of the energy equation has the phase-shifted sine form, parametrized by an arbitrary complex constant c. The two constant equilibrium solutions w=1/a and w=-1/a are included separately, and every listed family solves the equation.

General energy-equation solution family
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_13 (a : ℂ) (ha : a ≠ 0) : ∀ w : ℂ → ℂ, (Differentiable ℂ w ∧ (∀ z, (deriv w z) ^ 2 + a ^ 2 * (w z) ^ 2 = 1)) ↔ (∃ c : ℂ, ∀ z, w z = energyCandidate a c z) ∨ (∀ z, w z = 1 / a) ∨ (∀ z, w z = -(1 / a))
DLMF 4.20.14 ↗

w=\frac1a\tan(az+c).

On an open preconnected domain U, every nonconstant differentiable solution of the Riccati equation is a tangent family with parameter c, provided the domain avoids the finite poles. The constant equilibria w=I/a and w=-I/a are included separately; conversely each listed family solves the equation on U.

General Riccati solution family
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_20_14 (a : ℂ) (ha : a ≠ 0) (U : Set ℂ) (hUopen : IsOpen U) (hUconn : IsPreconnected U) : ∀ w : ℂ → ℂ, (DifferentiableOn ℂ w U ∧ (∀ z ∈ U, (derivWithin w U z) - a ^ 2 * (w z) ^ 2 = 1)) ↔ (∃ c : ℂ, (∀ z ∈ U, w z = riccatiCandidate a c z) ∧ (∀ z ∈ U, Complex.cos (a * z + c) ≠ 0)) ∨ (∀ z ∈ U, w z = Complex.I / a) ∨ (∀ z ∈ U, w z = -Complex.I / a)
