4.28. §4.28 Definitions and Periodicity🔗

Source: NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §4.28, Definitions and Periodicity.

Open this chapter in the DLMF ↗

§4.28 Definitions and Periodicity🔗

The variables in this section are complex. The first seven entries define the hyperbolic functions from the exponential, quotient, and reciprocal conventions. The next six identify hyperbolic functions with ordinary trigonometric functions evaluated at an imaginary argument. The reciprocal definitions use the usual totalized Lean division; away from zeros they are the analytic functions represented by the DLMF notation.

Definitions🔗

Source: DLMF §4.28, Definitions.

DLMF 4.28.1 ↗

\sinh z=\frac{e^z-e^{-z}}{2}.

Hyperbolic sine is the odd part of the exponential, with z allowed to be any complex number.

Hyperbolic sine definition
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_1 (z : ℂ) : Complex.sinh z = (Complex.exp z - Complex.exp (-z)) / 2
DLMF 4.28.2 ↗

\cosh z=\frac{e^z+e^{-z}}{2}.

Hyperbolic cosine is the even part of the exponential, on the same entire complex domain.

Hyperbolic cosine definition
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_2 (z : ℂ) : Complex.cosh z = (Complex.exp z + Complex.exp (-z)) / 2
DLMF 4.28.3 ↗

\cosh z\mathbin{\pm}\sinh z=e^{\pm z}.

The two signs are recorded together: the plus combination is exp z, and the minus combination is exp (-z).

Exponential combinations
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_3 (z : ℂ) : Complex.cosh z + Complex.sinh z = Complex.exp z ∧ Complex.cosh z - Complex.sinh z = Complex.exp (-z)
DLMF 4.28.4 ↗

\tanh z=\frac{\sinh z}{\cosh z}.

The hyperbolic tangent is the quotient of hyperbolic sine by hyperbolic cosine. Its poles occur where the denominator vanishes.

Hyperbolic tangent definition
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_4 (z : ℂ) : Complex.tanh z = Complex.sinh z / Complex.cosh z
DLMF 4.28.5 ↗

\operatorname{csch} z=\frac1{\sinh z}.

The hyperbolic cosecant is the reciprocal of hyperbolic sine, with the source domain understood away from the zeros of sinh.

Hyperbolic cosecant definition
def hyperbolicCosecant (z : ℂ) : ℂ := 1 / Complex.sinh z
DLMF 4.28.6 ↗

\operatorname{sech} z=\frac1{\cosh z}.

The hyperbolic secant is the reciprocal of hyperbolic cosine, away from its zero set.

Hyperbolic secant definition
def hyperbolicSecant (z : ℂ) : ℂ := 1 / Complex.cosh z
DLMF 4.28.7 ↗

\coth z=\frac1{\tanh z}.

The hyperbolic cotangent is the reciprocal of hyperbolic tangent, with the corresponding pole convention.

Hyperbolic cotangent definition
def hyperbolicCotangent (z : ℂ) : ℂ := 1 / Complex.tanh z

Relations to trigonometric functions🔗

Source: DLMF §4.28, Relations to Trigonometric Functions.

The imaginary-argument identities below are complex identities. In the cosecant and secant entries, Mathlib has no dedicated complex reciprocal function names, so the page uses the transparent local definitions complexCosecant z = 1/sin z and complexSecant z = 1/cos z.

DLMF 4.28.8 ↗

\sin(iz)=i\sinh z.

Sine at i z is i times hyperbolic sine.

Sine and hyperbolic sine
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_8 (z : ℂ) : Complex.sin (Complex.I * z) = Complex.I * Complex.sinh z
DLMF 4.28.9 ↗

\cos(iz)=\cosh z.

Cosine at i z is hyperbolic cosine without an additional factor.

Cosine and hyperbolic cosine
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_9 (z : ℂ) : Complex.cos (Complex.I * z) = Complex.cosh z
DLMF 4.28.10 ↗

\tan(iz)=i\tanh z.

The tangent identity follows the same imaginary-argument conversion, away from poles under the quotient convention.

Tangent and hyperbolic tangent
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_10 (z : ℂ) : Complex.tan (Complex.I * z) = Complex.I * Complex.tanh z
DLMF 4.28.11 ↗

\csc(iz)=-i\operatorname{csch} z.

Using the transparent reciprocal definitions, cosecant at i z becomes -i times hyperbolic cosecant.

Cosecant and hyperbolic cosecant
def complexCosecant (z : ℂ) : ℂ := 1 / Complex.sin ztheorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_11 (z : ℂ) : complexCosecant (Complex.I * z) = -Complex.I * hyperbolicCosecant z
DLMF 4.28.12 ↗

\sec(iz)=\operatorname{sech} z.

Secant at i z agrees with hyperbolic secant, again using reciprocal definitions on their pole-free domains.

Secant and hyperbolic secant
def complexSecant (z : ℂ) : ℂ := 1 / Complex.cos ztheorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_12 (z : ℂ) : complexSecant (Complex.I * z) = hyperbolicSecant z
DLMF 4.28.13 ↗

\cot(iz)=-i\coth z.

The cotangent identity is the reciprocal counterpart of E10.

Cotangent and hyperbolic cotangent
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_13 (z : ℂ) : Complex.cot (Complex.I * z) = -Complex.I * hyperbolicCotangent z

Periodicity and zeros🔗

Source: DLMF §4.28, Periodicity and Zeros.

The hyperbolic sine and cosine have period 2πi, while hyperbolic tangent has period πi. Their zeros are sinh z = 0 at z = i k π and cosh z = 0 at z = i(k+1/2)π, for integers k. The single Lean declaration records both periods and both zero families in one reusable proposition.

Hyperbolic periodicity and zero sets
theorem declaration uses `sorry`dlmf_4_28_periodicity_and_zeros : (∀ z : ℂ, Complex.sinh (z + 2 * (Real.pi : ℂ) * Complex.I) = Complex.sinh z) ∧ (∀ z : ℂ, Complex.cosh (z + 2 * (Real.pi : ℂ) * Complex.I) = Complex.cosh z) ∧ (∀ z : ℂ, Complex.tanh (z + (Real.pi : ℂ) * Complex.I) = Complex.tanh z) ∧ (∀ z : ℂ, Complex.sinh z = 0 ↔ ∃ k : ℤ, z = (k : ℂ) * (Real.pi : ℂ) * Complex.I) ∧ (∀ z : ℂ, Complex.cosh z = 0 ↔ ∃ k : ℤ, z = ((k : ℂ) + (1 / 2 : ℂ)) * (Real.pi : ℂ) * Complex.I)

The source notes that the hyperbolic identities inherit many properties from the corresponding trigonometric functions. For background references, it points to Hobson (1928), pp. 322–326, and Levinson and Redheffer (1970), pp. 56–57.
