## Scope and standing assumptions

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be arbitrary types, let \(D,D'\subseteq X\), and let \(E\) be a normed additive commutative group. All operations on functions below are pointwise. For example,

\[
(f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x),\qquad
(f-a)(x)=f(x)-a(x),
\]

and \(0\) denotes the zero function when it occurs in a function position.

Here \(f,a:X\to E\) and \(b:X\to\mathbb R\). No continuity, measurability, decidability, or nonemptiness assumption on \(D\) is involved.

<a id="qb-001-t01-erroron"></a>
### QB-001-T01 — `ErrorOn`

The first foundational predicate is

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall x\in D,\ \|f(x)-a(x)\|\le b(x),
\]

<a id="qb-001-t02-normboundon"></a>
### QB-001-T02 — `NormBoundOn`

The second foundational predicate is

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D;f;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall x\in D,\ \|f(x)\|\le b(x).
\]

<a id="qb-001-t03-haserrorfamily"></a>
### QB-001-T03 — `HasErrorFamily`

For order-indexed estimates, define the deliberately neutral family predicate

\[
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}(f;a,D,b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall n\in\mathbb N,\
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_n;f,a_n;b_n),
\]

where \(a_n:X\to E\), \(D_n\subseteq X\), and \(b_n:X\to\mathbb R\).

Despite the suggestive use of an order parameter, `HasErrorFamily` does **not** assert that \(a_n\) is a finite expansion, partial sum, truncation, or coefficient-generated expression. It also asserts no convergence in \(n\), no monotonic improvement of \(b_n\), and no compatibility between different indices. Those properties require separate definitions and proofs.

### Prospective supporting observation outside QB-001

A preliminary consequence used below is bound nonnegativity on the stated domain:

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\Longrightarrow
\forall x\in D,\ 0\le b(x),
\]

and similarly for `NormBoundOn`. Indeed, \(0\le\|f(x)-a(x)\|\le b(x)\). Therefore separate nonnegativity assumptions on a majorant are normally redundant when the corresponding bound predicate is already available. No conclusion is obtained about \(b\) outside \(D\).

## QB-001.1: Restriction and change of domain

<a id="qb-001-t05-erroron-restrict"></a>
### QB-001-T05 — `ErrorOn.restrict`

#### Statement

If \(D'\subseteq D\), then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D';f,a;b).
\]

#### Proof

Take \(x\in D'\). The inclusion gives \(x\in D\), so the original pointwise estimate applies.

### Prospective counterparts and observations outside QB-001

The same implication holds with `NormBoundOn` in place of `ErrorOn`.

If \(D=D'\), either predicate on \(D\) is equivalent to the corresponding predicate on \(D'\).

#### Proof of the prospective domain-equivalence counterpart

Domain equality gives inclusions in both directions.

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

The result remains valid when \(D'\) or \(D\) is empty. It is vacuous on an empty restricted domain. The reverse implication under a proper inclusion is false: an estimate on a smaller set gives no control at points newly added to the domain.

### Dependency and novelty

This is definitional predicate restriction plus set inclusion. It contains no norm inequality and no novel mathematics.

## QB-001.2: Weakening the majorant

<a id="qb-001-t06-erroron-weaken"></a>
### QB-001-T06 — `ErrorOn.weaken`

#### Statement

Assume

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\forall x\in D,\ b(x)\le d(x).
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;d).
\]

#### Proof

For \(x\in D\), concatenate the two real inequalities

\[
\|f(x)-a(x)\|\le b(x)\le d(x).
\]

### Prospective counterparts and observations outside QB-001

The analogous statement holds for `NormBoundOn`.

More generally, if \(D'\subseteq D\) and \(b(x)\le d(x)\) for every \(x\in D'\), then an estimate with \(b\) on \(D\) yields an estimate with \(d\) on \(D'\).

The combined version first restricts to \(D'\) and then uses the comparison there.

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

Only the values of the comparison on the conclusion domain matter. The direction is essential: a bound by \(b\) does not imply a bound by a smaller \(d\). Nonnegativity of \(d\) alone is not enough.

### Dependency and novelty

This uses only transitivity of the order on \(\mathbb R\). It is routine but not purely definitional.

## QB-001.3: Transitivity of error estimates

<a id="qb-001-t07-erroron-trans"></a>
### QB-001-T07 — `ErrorOn.trans`

#### Statement

If

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;a,c;d),
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,c;b+d).
\]

#### Proof

For \(x\) in the common domain, the exact additive identity

\[
f(x)-c(x)=(f(x)-a(x))+(a(x)-c(x))
\]

and the triangle inequality give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f(x)-c(x)\|
&\le \|f(x)-a(x)\|+\|a(x)-c(x)\|\\
&\le b(x)+d(x).
\end{aligned}
\]

### Prospective counterparts and observations outside QB-001

If the first premise holds on \(D_1\) and the second on \(D_2\), the same conclusion holds on \(D_1\cap D_2\).

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

The intermediate function must be the same \(a\) in the two premises. In general, neither \(\max(b,d)\) nor \(\min(b,d)\) can replace \(b+d\). There is no corresponding “transitivity” operation for `NormBoundOn`; its useful two-function rule is the additive triangle bound proved below.

### Dependency and novelty

This is the first genuine norm estimate in the packet. It depends on the additive identity and the triangle inequality, and is otherwise standard metric-style error calculus.

## QB-001.4: Symmetry

### Statement

For every \(D,f,a,b\),

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;a,f;b).
\]

### Proof

For every \(x\),

\[
a(x)-f(x)=-(f(x)-a(x)),
\]

and the norm is invariant under negation. Thus the two left-hand sides being bounded are equal pointwise.

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

No nonnegativity assumption on \(b\) needs to be added: either predicate, if true, already forces it on \(D\). `NormBoundOn` has no symmetry theorem because it has no approximant argument to exchange with the target.

### Dependency and novelty

This uses an exact additive identity and norm invariance under negation. It introduces no new inequality.

## QB-001.5: Exactness, zero error, and conversion to norm bounds

<a id="qb-001-t04-erroron-exact"></a>
### QB-001-T04 — `ErrorOn.exact`

#### Statement

If \(f=a\) on \(D\), then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;0).
\]

#### Proof

Under pointwise equality, the difference is zero and has norm zero.

`ErrorOn.exact` is the current frozen target name from the signature artifact.

### Prospective counterparts and observations outside QB-001

Conversely,

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;0)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall x\in D,\ f(x)=a(x).
\]

More generally, if \(f=a\) on \(D\), then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall x\in D,\ 0\le b(x).
\]

The two foundational predicates satisfy

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D;f-a;b),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D;f;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,0;b).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D;0;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0\le b\text{ on }D,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D;f;0)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f=0\text{ on }D.
\]

#### Prospective declaration names outside QB-001

The equivalences displayed in this section are prospective candidates for a
future card.  They are not QB-001 targets and are not part of the current
public API.  Possible descriptive names for later review are:

- `ErrorOn.eq_zero_iff` for zero error being equivalent to equality on \(D\);
- `ErrorOn.iff_nonneg_of_eqOn` for the arbitrary-majorant equivalence under equality on \(D\);
- `ErrorOn.iff_normBoundOn_sub` for conversion to a norm bound on the pointwise remainder;
- `NormBoundOn.iff_errorOn_zero` for conversion to an error estimate with zero approximant;
- `NormBoundOn.zero_iff_nonneg` for a bound on the zero function being equivalent to majorant nonnegativity on \(D\);
- `NormBoundOn.eq_zero_iff` for a zero norm bound being equivalent to equality with the zero function on \(D\).

These names are provisional.  A later card must decide which results, if any,
become public and must freeze their signatures and proof bindings separately.

#### Proofs of the prospective counterparts

Conversely, an error bounded by zero has nonnegative norm at most zero, hence norm exactly zero; definiteness of the norm then gives equality. The conversions between `ErrorOn` and `NormBoundOn` result from simplifying the pointwise identities \((f-a)(x)=f(x)-a(x)\) and \(f(x)-0=f(x)\).

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

Exact equality does not give an estimate with an arbitrary signed majorant: one still needs \(0\le b\) on the domain. The converse from zero norm to equality specifically uses a genuine normed group. In a non-separated seminormed group, norm zero need not imply equality. On an empty domain every displayed equivalence reduces to a vacuous pointwise assertion.

### Dependency and novelty

The predicate conversions are definitional after pointwise simplification. The zero-error converse additionally depends on nonnegativity and definiteness of the norm.

## QB-001.6: Congruence

### Statement

Suppose \(D=D'\), and on \(D\) suppose

\[
f=f',\qquad a=a',\qquad b=b'.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D';f',a';b').
\]

If \(D=D'\), \(f=f'\) on \(D\), and \(b=b'\) on \(D\), the analogous equivalence holds for `NormBoundOn`.

### Proof

At each point of the domain, substitute the assumed equalities into the defining inequality. Domain equality identifies the quantifier ranges.

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

Global function equality is unnecessary; equality on the domain suffices. A one-sided comparison \(b\le b'\) gives a one-way weakening theorem, not congruence. With only \(D'\subseteq D\), only the restriction direction follows.

### Dependency and novelty

This is substitution into a predicate. It is definitional infrastructure rather than a quantitative estimate.

## QB-001.7: Reparameterization by precomposition

<a id="qb-001-t08-erroron-comp"></a>
### QB-001-T08 — `ErrorOn.comp`

#### Statement

Let \(\psi:Y\to X\), let \(S\subseteq Y\), and suppose \(\psi(S)\subseteq D\). Then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{ErrorOn}
\bigl(S;f\circ\psi,a\circ\psi,b\circ\psi\bigr).
\]

#### Proof

If \(y\in S\), then \(\psi(y)\in D\). Apply the original estimate at \(\psi(y)\) and unfold composition.

### Prospective counterparts and observations outside QB-001

The same rule holds for `NormBoundOn`.

In particular, one may always take \(S=\psi^{-1}(D)\).

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

No injectivity, surjectivity, continuity, or differentiability of \(\psi\) is required for a value estimate. A converse requires enough surjectivity onto the desired domain and is not automatic.

This is precomposition. Arbitrary postcomposition is false: an estimate for \(f-a\) gives no estimate for \(\Phi(f)-\Phi(a)\) without quantitative control on \(\Phi\). An \(L\)-Lipschitz outer map gives an \(Lb\) bound, and a bounded linear map gives its explicit operator-norm factor.

### Dependency and novelty

This is definitional pullback along a map plus the set-mapping hypothesis. Derivative transport is a separate theorem requiring a chain rule and derivative bounds.

## QB-001.8: Addition, subtraction, and negation

### Error statements

If

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b),
\qquad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;g,c;d),
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f+g,a+c;b+d)
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f-g,a-c;b+d).
\]

Also,

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;-f,-a;b).
\]

If the two premises have domains \(D_1,D_2\), the binary conclusions hold on \(D_1\cap D_2\).

### Norm-bound statements

If

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D;f;b),
\qquad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D;g;d),
\]

then both \(f+g\) and \(f-g\) have norm bound \(b+d\) on \(D\). Negation preserves a norm bound unchanged.

### Proof

Use the exact identities

\[
(f+g)-(a+c)=(f-a)+(g-c),
\]

\[
(f-g)-(a-c)=(f-a)-(g-c),
\]

and

\[
(-f)-(-a)=-(f-a).
\]

The triangle inequalities for sums and differences prove the first two estimates; norm invariance under negation proves the third. The norm-bound versions are the same arguments with zero approximants.

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

The subtraction bound is \(b+d\), not \(b-d\). Cancellation can make a particular error smaller, but no such cancellation follows from separate norm bounds alone. Premises valid on different domains yield a conclusion only where both are valid.

### Dependency and novelty

Addition and subtraction are genuine applications of the triangle inequality. Negation is an exact norm identity. These are standard closure rules.

## QB-001.9: Scalar multiplication with an explicit norm factor

### Assumptions and statement

The scalar API uses exactly the typeclass assumptions `[NormedField 𝕂] [NormedAddCommGroup E] [NormedSpace 𝕂 E]`. In mathematical terms, \(\mathbb K\) is a normed field and \(E\) is a normed additive commutative group and normed space over \(\mathbb K\). For a fixed scalar \(\lambda\in\mathbb K\),

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{ErrorOn}
(D;\lambda f,\lambda a;\|\lambda\|b).
\]

The same statement holds for `NormBoundOn`.

More generally, for a fixed pointwise scalar multiplier \(\lambda:X\to\mathbb K\),

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{ErrorOn}
\left(
D;
x\mapsto\lambda(x)f(x),
x\mapsto\lambda(x)a(x),
x\mapsto\|\lambda(x)\|b(x)
\right).
\]

### Proof

For each \(x\in D\), scalar distributivity and the norm-scaling law give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\lambda(x)f(x)-\lambda(x)a(x)\|
&=\|\lambda(x)(f(x)-a(x))\|\\
&=\|\lambda(x)\|\,\|f(x)-a(x)\|\\
&\le \|\lambda(x)\|b(x).
\end{aligned}
\]

The last step uses the nonnegativity of \(\|\lambda(x)\|\).

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

The theorem includes \(\lambda=0\), where the resulting bound is zero. No uniform bound on a pointwise multiplier is required for the pointwise conclusion.

The unchanged majorant \(b\) is valid only with an additional contraction condition such as \(\|\lambda(x)\|\le1\) on \(D\). If the scalar is itself approximated rather than copied exactly to the approximant, this lemma does not apply; that situation is a product or bilinear error estimate.

### Dependency and novelty

This depends on scalar distributivity, the norm-scaling law, and monotonicity of multiplication by a nonnegative real. The visible factor \(\|\lambda\|\) is part of the quantitative contract.

## QB-001.10: Finite sums

### Statement

Let \(\iota\) be an arbitrary index type and let \(S:\operatorname{Finset}\iota\). Suppose that, for every \(i\in S\),

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f_i,a_i;b_i).
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}
\left(
D;
\sum_{i\in S}f_i,
\sum_{i\in S}a_i,
\sum_{i\in S}b_i
\right).
\]

Likewise, norm bounds \(b_i\) for \(f_i\) give the norm bound \(\sum_{i\in S}b_i\) for \(\sum_{i\in S}f_i\).

For the weighted theorem, additionally assume exactly `[NormedField 𝕂] [NormedAddCommGroup E] [NormedSpace 𝕂 E]`, and let

\[
\lambda_i:X\to\mathbb K\qquad(i\in\iota).
\]

If \(\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f_i,a_i;b_i)\) holds for every \(i\in S\), then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}
\left(
D;
x\mapsto\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i(x)\,\mathbin{\bullet}\,f_i(x),
x\mapsto\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i(x)\,\mathbin{\bullet}\,a_i(x),
x\mapsto\sum_{i\in S}\|\lambda_i(x)\|b_i(x)
\right),
\]

where \(\mathbin{\bullet}\) denotes scalar multiplication. Equivalently, for every \(x\in D\),

\[
\left\|
\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i(x)\mathbin{\bullet}f_i(x)
-
\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i(x)\mathbin{\bullet}a_i(x)
\right\|
\le
\sum_{i\in S}\|\lambda_i(x)\|b_i(x).
\]

### Proof

At each \(x\in D\), first use the exact finite-sum identity

\[
\sum_{i\in S}f_i(x)-\sum_{i\in S}a_i(x)
=
\sum_{i\in S}(f_i(x)-a_i(x)).
\]

Repeated triangle inequality gives

\[
\left\|\sum_{i\in S}(f_i(x)-a_i(x))\right\|
\le
\sum_{i\in S}\|f_i(x)-a_i(x)\|.
\]

Sum the componentwise inequalities to obtain the claimed bound. The weighted version first applies scalar multiplication to each summand.

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

The empty sum is included and gives the zero function with zero bound. A finite index type is unnecessary; only the selected index set must be finite. This theorem says nothing about infinite sums: an infinite analogue requires summability and a justified passage to the limit.

### Dependency and novelty

The exact sum rearrangement is algebraic. The estimate is finite induction using the triangle inequality and monotonicity of finite sums. It is standard but central reusable infrastructure.

## QB-001.11: Pointwise minimum of two bounds

### Statement

If

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;b)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;d),
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D;f,a;\min(b,d)),
\]

where the minimum is pointwise. The analogous result holds for `NormBoundOn`.

If the two estimates hold on \(D_1\) and \(D_2\), respectively, the pointwise-minimum estimate holds on \(D_1\cap D_2\).

### Proof

For \(x\) in the common domain, the same real number \(\|f(x)-a(x)\|\) is at most both \(b(x)\) and \(d(x)\). A real number bounded above by both operands is bounded above by their minimum.

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

Both hypotheses must bound the same error quantity: the target, approximant, and point must agree. One estimate with bound \(b\), even together with \(d\ge0\), does not justify replacing \(b\) by \(\min(b,d)\). Bounds for different approximants also cannot be minimized without a further theorem relating those errors. No continuity or measurability of either majorant is required.

### Dependency and novelty

This is an order-lattice inference in \(\mathbb R\), not a norm inequality. It is routine but its hypotheses are easy to state incorrectly.

## QB-001.12: Products in normed rings and algebras

### Assumptions and principal statement

Let \(R\) be a type with a `NonUnitalNormedRing` structure, and let \(f,a,g,c:X\to R\). Thus multiplication may be noncommutative and no multiplicative identity is assumed. Completeness is not used.

Assume

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_e;f,a;e),
\qquad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_d;g,c;d),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_M;a;M),
\qquad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_L;c;L).
\]

Set

\[
\Omega=D_e\cap D_d\cap D_M\cap D_L.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}
\bigl(\Omega;fg,ac;eL+Md+ed\bigr).
\]

All products in the conclusion majorant are products of real-valued functions restricted to \(\Omega\). The common-domain theorem is the specialization \(D_e=D_d=D_M=D_L=D\). The same result applies to internal multiplication in a normed algebra whose underlying multiplication supplies a `NonUnitalNormedRing` structure.

### Proof

At a fixed \(x\in\Omega\), membership in the fourfold intersection supplies all four premises. Suppress the argument. The exact identity

\[
fg-ac=(f-a)(g-c)+(f-a)c+a(g-c)
\]

is valid without commutativity. Apply the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|fg-ac\|
&\le
\|(f-a)(g-c)\|
+\|(f-a)c\|
+\|a(g-c)\|\\
&\le
\|f-a\|\,\|g-c\|
+\|f-a\|\,\|c\|
+\|a\|\,\|g-c\|\\
&\le ed+eL+Md.
\end{aligned}
\]

The premises imply that \(e,d,M,L\) are nonnegative on \(\Omega\), so the component inequalities may be multiplied. Thus no redundant standalone nonnegativity premises are needed in the packaged theorem. If the last arithmetic step is extracted as a lemma about otherwise unconstrained real variables, nonnegativity must be stated there.

### Sharper variants

The first sharper variant has the following complete premises:

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_e;f,a;e),
\qquad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_d;g,c;d),
\]

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_M;a;M),
\qquad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_N;g;N).
\]

On

\[
\Omega_1=D_e\cap D_d\cap D_M\cap D_N,
\]

the shorter identity

\[
fg-ac=(f-a)g+a(g-c)
\]

gives

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(\Omega_1;fg,ac;eN+Md).
\]

The second sharper variant has the complete premises

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_e;f,a;e),
\qquad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_d;g,c;d),
\]

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_P;f;P),
\qquad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_L;c;L).
\]

On

\[
\Omega_2=D_e\cap D_d\cap D_P\cap D_L,
\]

the identity

\[
fg-ac=f(g-c)+(f-a)c
\]

gives

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(\Omega_2;fg,ac;Pd+eL).
\]

These two estimates and the approximant-only estimate are mathematically
distinct prospective results outside QB-001: none dominates the others without
comparisons among the supplied norm bounds.  None is a current target or a
frozen public declaration.

If

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_b;f;b),
\qquad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_d;g;d),
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_b\cap D_d;fg;bd)
\]

by submultiplicativity.

### General biadditive estimate

Let \(U,V,W\) be normed additive commutative groups, and let

\[
\beta:U\times V\to W
\]

be biadditive: for every fixed \(v\in V\), the map \(u\mapsto\beta(u,v)\) is additive, and for every fixed \(u\in U\), the map \(v\mapsto\beta(u,v)\) is additive. Suppose there is a real constant \(K\) such that

\[
0\le K,
\qquad
\forall u\in U\ \forall v\in V,\quad
\|\beta(u,v)\|\le K\|u\|\|v\|,
\]

and let

\[
f,a:X\to U,
\qquad
g,c:X\to V.
\]

Assume

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_e;f,a;e),
\qquad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_d;g,c;d),
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_M;a;M),
\qquad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D_L;c;L).
\]

For \(x\in X\), define

\[
B(f,g)(x)=\beta(f(x),g(x)),
\qquad
B(a,c)(x)=\beta(a(x),c(x)).
\]

Then, on \(\Omega=D_e\cap D_d\cap D_M\cap D_L\),

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}
\bigl(\Omega;B(f,g),B(a,c);K(eL+Md+ed)\bigr).
\]

For every \(x\in X\), biadditivity gives the explicitly pointwise identity

\[
B(f,g)(x)-B(a,c)(x)
=
\beta(f(x)-a(x),g(x)-c(x))
+\beta(f(x)-a(x),c(x))
+\beta(a(x),g(x)-c(x)).
\]

For \(x\in\Omega\), the triangle inequality and the displayed norm control for \(\beta\) then give the result exactly as in the product proof. Internal multiplication in a `NonUnitalNormedRing` is the case \(U=V=W=R\), \(\beta(u,v)=uv\), and \(K=1\).

### Edge cases and false nearby statements

The cross term \(ed\) cannot in general be dropped when only approximant norms are known. Here is a concrete counterexample. Take \(X=\mathrm{Unit}\), every premise domain equal to \(\mathrm{univ}\), and \(R=\mathbb R\). Let \(f=g=1\), \(a=c=0\), \(e=d=1\), and \(M=L=0\), all as constant functions. Every premise holds. The product error is

\[
|1\cdot1-0\cdot0|=1,
\]

whereas the proposed bound without the cross term is

\[
eL+Md=1\cdot0+0\cdot1=0.
\]

Thus omitting \(ed\) produces a false theorem.

No commutativity is hidden in the proof: the order of factors in the exact identity is preserved. Every product conclusion above explicitly uses the intersection of all domains supporting premises used by that conclusion.

### Dependency and novelty

This depends on distributivity, the additive triangle inequality, norm submultiplicativity, and monotonicity of multiplication on nonnegative reals. It is standard perturbation algebra, but among the MVP rules it is the most substantive inequality and the easiest place to lose a necessary contribution.

## QB-001.13: `HasErrorFamily` wrappers

Write

\[
(D\cap E)_n=D_n\cap E_n,
\qquad
(a\pm c)_n=a_n\pm c_n,
\qquad
(b+d)_n=b_n+d_n.
\]

Every result in this section follows by fixing \(n\) and applying the corresponding `ErrorOn` theorem. The wrappers add no new analytic content.

### Restriction and weakening

If `HasErrorFamily` holds on \(D_n\) and \(D'_n\subseteq D_n\) for every \(n\), it holds on \(D'_n\) with the same approximants and bounds.

If `HasErrorFamily` holds with \(b_n\), and

\[
\forall n\ \forall x\in D_n,\quad b_n(x)\le d_n(x),
\]

it holds with \(d_n\).

### Exactness and congruence

If

\[
\forall n\ \forall x\in D_n,\quad f(x)=a_n(x),
\]

then `HasErrorFamily` holds with the zero bound family.

Pointwise equality, for every \(n\), of the domains, target values on the domain, approximant values on the domain, and bound values on the domain preserves `HasErrorFamily` in both directions.

### Transitivity

Suppose

\[
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}(f;a,D,b)
\]

and, for every \(n\),

\[
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(E_n;a_n,c_n;d_n).
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}
(f;c,D\cap E,b+d).
\]

The second premise is intentionally an indexed family of `ErrorOn` statements rather than another `HasErrorFamily`: its target \(a_n\) varies with \(n\).

### Reparameterization

Let \(\psi:Y\to X\) be fixed independently of \(n\), and suppose

\[
\psi(S_n)\subseteq D_n
\]

for every \(n\). Then an error family for \(f\) pulls back to one for \(f\circ\psi\), with approximants \(a_n\circ\psi\), domains \(S_n\), and bounds \(b_n\circ\psi\).

### Addition, subtraction, and negation

If

\[
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}(f;a,D,b)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}(g;c,E,d),
\]

then the sum and difference families have domains

\[
\Omega_n=D_n\cap E_n,
\]

approximants \(a_n+c_n\) and \(a_n-c_n\), respectively, and bound \(b_n+d_n\). Thus every premise domain is explicitly represented in the conclusion.

An error family for \(f\) yields one for \(-f\), with approximants \(-a_n\) and unchanged bounds.

### Scalar multiplication

Assume exactly `[NormedField 𝕂] [NormedAddCommGroup E] [NormedSpace 𝕂 E]`. A fixed scalar, or a fixed pointwise multiplier \(\lambda:X\to\mathbb K\) independent of \(n\), multiplies the target and every approximant. The new bound at \((n,x)\) is

\[
\|\lambda(x)\|b_n(x).
\]

If the multiplier depends on \(n\), the resulting target \(x\mapsto\lambda_n(x)f(x)\) generally varies with \(n\) and therefore does not fit `HasErrorFamily` as defined.

### Finite sums

For an arbitrary index type \(\iota\) and \(S:\operatorname{Finset}\iota\), error families for fixed targets \(f_i\) combine into an error family for \(\sum_{i\in S}f_i\). With a common domain family, the approximant and bound at order \(n\) are

\[
\sum_{i\in S}a_{i,n}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sum_{i\in S}b_{i,n}.
\]

With differing domains, the order-\(n\) conclusion domain is explicitly

\[
\Omega_n=\bigcap_{i\in S}D_{i,n}.
\]

For weighted families, under the scalar assumptions above and with fixed multipliers \(\lambda_i:X\to\mathbb K\), the approximant and bound at order \(n\) are

\[
x\mapsto\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_i(x)\mathbin{\bullet}a_{i,n}(x)
\quad\text{and}\quad
x\mapsto\sum_{i\in S}\|\lambda_i(x)\|b_{i,n}(x),
\]

on \(\Omega_n\).

### Pointwise minimum

If the same fixed target and approximants have bound families \(b_n\) on \(D_n\) and \(d_n\) on \(E_n\), they have the pointwise-minimum bound family on the explicit domain

\[
\Omega_n=D_n\cap E_n.
\]

### Products

Let \(R\) have a `NonUnitalNormedRing` structure. Suppose

\[
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}(f;a,D^e,e),
\qquad
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}(g;c,D^d,d),
\]

and suppose, for every \(n\),

\[
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D^M_n;a_n;M_n),
\qquad
\operatorname{NormBoundOn}(D^L_n;c_n;L_n).
\]

Define the orderwise intersection of **all four** premise domains by

\[
\Omega_n=D^e_n\cap D^d_n\cap D^M_n\cap D^L_n.
\]

Then \(fg\) has an error family on \(\Omega_n\), with approximant \(a_nc_n\) and bound

\[
e_nL_n+M_nd_n+e_nd_n.
\]

In full,

\[
\operatorname{HasErrorFamily}
\left(
fg;
n\mapsto a_nc_n,
n\mapsto\Omega_n,
n\mapsto(e_nL_n+M_nd_n+e_nd_n)
\right).
\]

The two sharper product wrappers use their own complete orderwise intersections. With norm bounds \(M_n\) for \(a_n\) on \(D^M_n\) and \(N_n\) for the actual target \(g\) on \(D^N_n\), the first sharper bound is

\[
e_nN_n+M_nd_n
\]

on


\[
D^e_n\cap D^d_n\cap D^M_n\cap D^N_n.
\]

Thus the first sharper wrapper is `HasErrorFamily` with approximants \(a_nc_n\), those orderwise domains, and the bound family \(n\mapsto e_nN_n+M_nd_n\).

With norm bounds \(P_n\) for the actual target \(f\) on \(D^P_n\) and \(L_n\) for \(c_n\) on \(D^L_n\), the second sharper bound is

\[
P_nd_n+e_nL_n
\]

on

\[
D^e_n\cap D^d_n\cap D^P_n\cap D^L_n.
\]

Thus the second sharper wrapper is `HasErrorFamily` with approximants \(a_nc_n\), those orderwise domains, and the bound family \(n\mapsto P_nd_n+e_nL_n\).

### Symmetry caveat

There is no unrestricted `HasErrorFamily` symmetry wrapper. Pointwise symmetry gives

\[
\forall n,\quad
\operatorname{ErrorOn}(D_n;a_n,f;b_n),
\]

but \(a_n\) is generally an index-dependent target, whereas `HasErrorFamily` requires one fixed target. If all \(a_n\) are one fixed function \(a\), symmetry does give an error family for target \(a\) with constant approximant family \(f\).

### Semantic edge case

An all-orders `HasErrorFamily` theorem remains only an all-orders family of finite inequalities. It does not imply \(a_n\to f\), \(b_n\to0\), eventual domain membership, monotone error improvement, or that any \(a_n\) is a finite expansion. In particular, it is compatible with divergent asymptotic coefficient families and with bounds that worsen after some order.

## Consolidated false-nearby-statements checklist

The following claims must not enter the public API without the stated repair:

1. **Extending a domain:** a bound on \(D'\subsetneq D\) does not give a bound on \(D\). Restrict, do not extend.
2. **Strengthening a majorant:** \(\|r\|\le b\) and \(d\le b\) do not imply \(\|r\|\le d\). Weakening requires \(b\le d\).
3. **Exactness with a signed bound:** \(f=a\) does not imply an estimate with arbitrary \(b\); require \(b\ge0\) on the domain or use the zero bound.
4. **Zero error in a seminorm:** norm-zero implies equality only in a separated normed space, not an arbitrary seminormed space.
5. **Transitivity with a maximum:** two successive errors generally add; their maximum is insufficient.
6. **Minimum from one estimate:** nonnegativity of a second candidate majorant does not make its minimum with a valid bound valid. Both bounds must control the same error.
7. **Subtraction with a difference of bounds:** separate error controls give \(b+d\), not \(b-d\).
8. **Scalar multiplication without loss:** the factor \(\|\lambda\|\) cannot be omitted unless a contraction or sharper special property is proved.
9. **Arbitrary outer composition:** precomposition is automatic; postcomposition requires Lipschitz, linear, derivative, or other quantitative control.
10. **Product without the cross term:** approximant norm bounds alone give \(eL+Md+ed\); dropping \(ed\) is false.
11. **Implicit commutativity in products:** the product proof must preserve factor order; commutativity is unnecessary and should not be assumed.
12. **Infinite sums by finite induction:** the finite-sum theorem provides no infinite-sum result without summability and limit control.
13. **Family symmetry:** reversing each indexed error usually produces index-dependent targets and is not another `HasErrorFamily`.
14. **Family equals expansion:** `HasErrorFamily` supplies no finiteness, coefficient representation, convergence, or order-improvement property.
