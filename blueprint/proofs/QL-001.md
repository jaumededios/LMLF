# QL-001: finite local-tail bound for Laplace remainders

## Status and scope

This is the first complete natural-language proof draft. It is not a frozen
theorem specification and authorizes no Lean implementation. The exact Lean
signature is intentionally withheld until independent review chooses the
improper-integral and scalar interfaces.

The mathematical packet has two targets:

1. a generic local-plus-tail estimate for a remainder \(\phi\), expressed in
   terms of supplied constants \(k,K,X,L,\beta\);
2. a finite Watson decomposition obtained after evaluating the finitely many
   model moments.

The packet does not derive numerical values of \(k,K,X,L\) for an arbitrary
amplitude. Concrete named-function applications must supply or bound them.

## Standing data and quantifier ledger

Let \(\mathbb K\) be either \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), pending source and
API review. Norms below are absolute value or complex norm. All powers of
positive real variables use `Real.rpow` semantics.

| ID | Data | Quantification and dependence | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| QL-D01 | \(\phi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb K\) | fixed | remainder amplitude |
| QL-D02 | \(\beta>0\) | fixed | local power exponent plus one |
| QL-D03 | \(k>0\) | fixed | local/tail split point |
| QL-D04 | \(K\ge0\) | fixed | local power-law majorant |
| QL-D05 | \(X\in\mathbb R\) | fixed | baseline for the weighted primitive |
| QL-D06 | \(L\ge0\) | fixed | uniform bound for the primitive |
| QL-D07 | \(x>\max(X,0)\) | theorem input | requested Laplace parameter |
| QL-D08 | \(n\in\mathbb N\) | fixed for T02 | number of retained model terms |
| QL-D09 | \(\lambda,\mu>0\) | fixed for T02 | exponent offset and spacing |
| QL-D10 | \(a_s\in\mathbb K\) | fixed sequence | model coefficients |

No constant is uniform in data not explicitly listed. In the source
application, \(k,K,X,L\) may depend on \(n,q,a,\lambda,\mu\).

## Improper-integral convention required by this proof

For a locally integrable \(f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb K\), write

\[
 \operatorname{HasImp}_{[a,\infty)}(f;I)
\]

when

\[
 \lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_a^R f(t)\,dt=I.
\]

When the endpoint \(0\) can be singular, the full \((0,\infty)\) integral is
represented by a compatible two-sided limit, or by a split at a positive
\(k\): a lower-end limit on \((0,k]\) plus an upper-tail limit on
\([k,\infty)\). The packet must not identify this predicate with Mathlib's
totalized set integral unless absolute integrability has been proved.

For the finite theorem below, the local power majorant supplies absolute
integrability at zero. Conditional convergence is therefore needed only for
the tail.

## Exact preliminary identities

### QL-001-I01 - exponent normalization

For \(s,n\in\mathbb N\), set

\[
 \beta_s=\frac{s+\lambda}{\mu},
 \qquad
 \alpha_s=\frac{s+\lambda-\mu}{\mu}=\beta_s-1.
\]

Because \(\lambda,\mu>0\), every \(\beta_s>0\), hence
\(\alpha_s>-1\). In particular, the model powers are integrable at zero.

### QL-001-I02 - model moment

For \(x>0\),

\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-xt}t^{\beta_s-1}\,dt
 =\Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}. \tag{QL.1}
\]

This is the positive-real specialization of the pinned Mathlib theorem
`integral_rpow_mul_exp_neg_mul_Ioi`. Its printed right-hand side is
\((1/x)^{\beta_s}\Gamma(\beta_s)\), equal to the displayed form for \(x>0\).

### QL-001-I03 - finite decomposition

Define

\[
 p_n(t)=\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}a_st^{\beta_s-1},
 \qquad \phi_n(t)=q(t)-p_n(t).
\]

Whenever the relevant improper integrals exist, finite linearity and QL.1 give

\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-xt}q(t)\,dt
 -\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}a_s\Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}
 =\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}\phi_n(t)\,dt. \tag{QL.2}
\]

This is an identity, not an estimate. At \(n=0\), both finite sums are empty
and \(\phi_0=q\).

<a id="target-ql-001-t01"></a>
## QL-001-T01 - generic local-tail estimate

### Hypotheses

Assume:

1. \(\phi\) is locally integrable on every compact subinterval of
   \((0,\infty)\);
2. \(\beta>0\), \(k>0\), \(K\ge0\), and
   \[
     \lVert\phi(t)\rVert\le Kt^{\beta-1}
     \qquad(0<t\le k); \tag{QL.3}
   \]
3. define the baseline primitive
   \[
     F(t)=\int_k^t e^{-Xv}\phi(v)\,dv;
   \]
4. \(\lVert F(t)\rVert\le L\) for all \(t\ge k\), with \(L\ge0\);
5. \(x>\max(X,0)\).

### Conclusion

The improper Laplace integral of \(\phi\) exists at \(x\), and

\[
 \left\lVert\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}\phi(t)\,dt\right\rVert
 \le K\Gamma(\beta)x^{-\beta}
   +Le^{-(x-X)k}. \tag{QL.4}
\]

### Proof: local interval

By QL.3 and \(x>0\),

\[
 \lVert e^{-xt}\phi(t)\rVert
 \le K e^{-xt}t^{\beta-1}
 \qquad(0<t\le k).
\]

The right side is integrable at zero because \(\beta>0\). Norm-of-integral
and monotonicity give

\[
 \left\lVert\int_0^k e^{-xt}\phi(t)\,dt\right\rVert
 \le K\int_0^k e^{-xt}t^{\beta-1}\,dt
 \le K\Gamma(\beta)x^{-\beta}. \tag{QL.5}
\]

The second inequality enlarges the nonnegative integral to \((0,\infty)\) and
uses QL.1.

### Proof: tail identity on a finite interval

Put \(h=x-X>0\). Then

\[
 e^{-xt}\phi(t)=e^{-ht}\,e^{-Xt}\phi(t)=e^{-ht}F'(t)
\]

at points where the derivative representation holds. Finite-interval
integration by parts gives, for \(R\ge k\),

\[
 \int_k^R e^{-xt}\phi(t)\,dt
 =e^{-hR}F(R)+h\int_k^R e^{-ht}F(t)\,dt, \tag{QL.6}
\]

because \(F(k)=0\).

This step needs only the standard absolutely-continuous primitive theorem for
locally integrable functions, not pointwise differentiability everywhere. The
exact Lean route must be selected during API review.

### Proof: passage to infinity and tail bound

Since \(F\) is bounded by \(L\),

\[
 \lVert e^{-hR}F(R)\rVert\le Le^{-hR}\to0.
\]

Also,

\[
 \left\lVert h\int_k^R e^{-ht}F(t)\,dt\right\rVert
 \le hL\int_k^R e^{-ht}\,dt
 \le Le^{-hk}. \tag{QL.7}
\]

The function \(hLe^{-ht}\) is integrable on \([k,\infty)\), so dominated
convergence shows that the right side of QL.6 has a limit. Thus the tail
improper integral exists and

\[
 \left\lVert\int_k^\infty e^{-xt}\phi(t)\,dt\right\rVert
 \le Le^{-hk}. \tag{QL.8}
\]

Adding QL.5 and QL.8 proves QL.4.

### Why the hypotheses do not contain the conclusion

The primitive bound is imposed at one baseline weight \(X\), not at the target
weight \(x\), and contains no requested Laplace integral or target error. It is
strictly reusable: conditional convergence at \(X\) implies existence of some
finite \(L\), while concrete applications may provide a computable bound on
\(L\).

<a id="target-ql-001-t02"></a>
## QL-001-T02 - finite Watson remainder bound

Fix \(n,\lambda,\mu,a,q\) and set \(\beta_s, p_n,\phi_n\) as above. Assume the
T01 hypotheses for \(\phi_n\) with
\(\beta=\beta_n=(n+\lambda)/\mu\). Then QL.2 and QL.4 give

\[
 \left\lVert
 \int_0^\infty e^{-xt}q(t)\,dt
 -\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}
   a_s\Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}
 \right\rVert
 \le K\Gamma(\beta_n)x^{-\beta_n}
   +Le^{-(x-X)k}. \tag{QL.9}
\]

This is the reusable finite theorem needed by OLV-001. It is valid at \(n=0\)
with an empty approximant.

## Nonemptiness and regressions

### Exact model

Take \(q(t)=a_0t^{\beta_0-1}\), \(n=1\), and \(\phi_1=0\), where
\(\beta_0=\lambda/\mu\). Choose any \(k>0\), \(K=L=0\), and \(X=0\).
QL.9 becomes the exact Gamma moment.

### Order zero

At \(n=0\), QL.9 bounds the whole integral of \(q\). No access to \(a_0\) is
required by the finite sum, although a source-derived local bound may naturally
use the next coefficient.

### Negative majorants

Neither \(K<0\) nor \(L<0\) is accepted. Their nonnegativity is required before
monotonicity and norm bounds.

### Missing tail control

Fix any local model on \((0,k]\). Add to \(q\) an arbitrarily large
nonnegative bump supported in \([k+1,k+2]\). The local expansion is unchanged,
but the Laplace integral at a fixed \(x\) changes arbitrarily. Thus local data
alone cannot imply QL.9.

### Eventual convergence is not a uniform numeric bound

For a family indexed by \(A>0\), add \(A\) times the same remote bump. Every
member has an eventually convergent Laplace integral, but no common numeric
\(L\) follows. Uniform bounds require an explicit family hypothesis.

### Boundary \(x=X\)

At \(x=X\), \(h=0\) and the bounded-primitive argument no longer supplies
decay. Strict \(x>X\) is essential.
