# IMP-001: finite-exceptional improper integration dossier

## Status and exact boundary

This is the fourth complete author draft for the provisional, unregistered
`IMP-001` infrastructure packet. It is source-independent, earns no Olver
coverage, is not frozen, has requested changes pending fresh exact-commit rereview,
and authorizes no Lean declaration. Every declaration name in the frontmatter is prospective.
Revision 4 retains the complete revision-3 mathematics and repairs the public
construction defect identified by exact-signature referee B. The public target
T06 is now a representation-independent finite-exception composition theorem;
the finite-piece Abel identity becomes its private proof lemma. An R4 exact
Lean signature artifact is deliberately absent and may be designed only after
two fresh independent approvals of this proof and API/dependency architecture.
The revision-3 signature artifact and its reviews bind superseded bytes and
provide design evidence only.

The packet still has six public targets and no hidden quantitative theorem. It fixes
the mathematical meaning of a finite-exceptional ordinary improper integral on
a right ray, proves the three relation laws needed by the Watson path, fixes a
normalized continuous primitive predicate, and proves one finite-interval Abel
finite-exception composition theorem at an exact explicitly named value. The private proof
derives the finite-interval identity by Fubini, assembles it across arbitrary
finite `S`, and passes to infinity using two explicit weighted-tail hypotheses.
It does not prove the bounded-primitive tail estimate: that is `QL-001`. It
does not discuss Gamma moments or source recovery.

The interface follows the provisional D1--D5 disposition in
`review/questions/OLV97-C03-WATSON.md`: regular finite pieces are Mathlib
Bochner `intervalIntegral`s with explicit `IntervalIntegrable` evidence, while
only the finite exceptional endpoints and positive infinity are limits. No
second proper-Riemann library and no principal-value convention are introduced.

## Precise relational semantics

Write the increasing enumeration of \(S\) as

\[
 c_1<\cdots<c_m,\qquad k<c_1.
\]

This enumeration is unique because \(S\) is a finite set in a linear order.
For a bounded open component \((c_i,c_{i+1})\), use the displayed deterministic
anchor \(d_i=(c_i+c_{i+1})/2\). For the last component after \(c_m\), use
\(d_m=c_m+1\). These formulas are a mathematical presentation, not a demand
that Lean store a sorted list or these anchors.

Every integral below is `intervalIntegral` and is accompanied by
`IntervalIntegrable` on its compact interval. The one-sided filters remain
inside the indicated regular component.

- If \(m=0\), require a value \(I_0\) with

  \[
    \int_k^R f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow I_0\quad(R\to+\infty),
  \]

  and set \(I=I_0\).
- If \(m>0\), require a first-component value \(I_0\) with

  \[
    \int_k^b f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow I_0\quad(b\uparrow c_1).
  \]
- For \(1\le i<m\), require independent values \(L_i,R_i\) with

  \[
   \int_a^{d_i}f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow L_i\quad(a\downarrow c_i),
   \qquad
   \int_{d_i}^{b}f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow R_i\quad(b\uparrow c_{i+1}),
  \]

  and define that component's contribution to be \(L_i+R_i\).
- For the last component, require independent values \(L_m,R_m\) with

  \[
   \int_a^{d_m}f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow L_m\quad(a\downarrow c_m),
   \qquad
   \int_{d_m}^{R}f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow R_m\quad(R\to+\infty),
  \]

  and define its contribution to be \(L_m+R_m\).

The relation `HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept f k S I` means that all these
regular-piece integrability statements and limits hold and that

\[
 I=I_0+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(L_i+R_i)+(L_m+R_m)                 \tag{IMP.1}
\]

when \(m>0\), with the evident empty-set formula when \(m=0\). In particular,
the left and right contributions at \(c_i\) are not limits along a common
cutoff. The value \(f(c_i)\) itself is immaterial, as expected for Lebesgue
finite-piece integrals, but divergent approach from either side is not
immaterial.

### `IMP-001-I01` - anchor independence

Let \(l<d<e<r\) lie in one regular component. For truncation points
\(l<a<d<e<b<r\), interval additivity gives

\[
 \int_a^e f=\int_a^d f+\int_d^e f,
 \qquad
 \int_e^b f=\int_d^b f-\int_d^e f.                      \tag{IMP.2}
\]

The fixed compact integral \(\int_d^e f\) exists by regular-piece
`IntervalIntegrable`. Passing separately to \(a\downarrow l\) and
\(b\uparrow r\) shows that changing the anchor from \(d\) to \(e\) adds
\(\int_d^e f\) to the left contribution and subtracts the same vector from the
right contribution. Their sum is unchanged. The identical calculation applies
to the final unbounded component; its right endpoint limit is at infinity.
Thus IMP.1 is independent of the displayed anchors. This also proves that an
eventual Lean representation may use a sorted list, adjacent intervals, or a
component record without changing the mathematical relation.

### `IMP-001-I02` - insertion of a regular marked point

Suppose \(d\notin S\), \(k<d\), and \(d\) lies inside one regular component.
Because \(S\) is finite, choose \(p<d<q\) in that same component. The T01
regularity clause gives `IntervalIntegrable f volume p q`. Define

\[
 P(x)=\int_p^x f(t)\,dt.
\]

The exact pinned theorem
`intervalIntegral.continuousOn_primitive_interval'` (`IMP-M09`), applied with
the anchor \(p\in[[p,q]]\), makes \(P\) continuous on the unoriented interval
\([[p,q]]=[p,q]\). Consequently the restrictions of \(P\) to \((p,d)\) and
\((d,q)\) tend separately to the same value \(P(d)\). For fixed regular
anchors \(u<d<v\) in the old component, interval additivity rewrites the two
variable-endpoint integrals as

\[
 \int_u^x f=P(x)-P(u)\longrightarrow P(d)-P(u)=\int_u^d f
 \quad(x\uparrow d),                                    \tag{IMP.2a}
\]

\[
 \int_x^v f=P(v)-P(x)\longrightarrow P(v)-P(d)=\int_d^v f
 \quad(x\downarrow d).                                  \tag{IMP.2b}
\]

Thus insertion produces the two **individual** one-sided endpoint limits at
\(d\); it does not merely prove that their sum exists. We now compare the old
and refined component decompositions in every possible position.

1. **Insertion into the sole component when \(S=\varnothing\).** The old
   component is \([k,\infty)\). Equation IMP.2a gives the new first-component
   limit \(\int_k^d f\), and IMP.2b gives the finite left contribution of the
   new final component. For any fixed \(v>d\), additivity gives

   \[
    \int_k^R f=\int_k^d f+\int_d^v f+\int_v^R f.
   \]

   The old \(R\to\infty\) limit therefore gives the new final outer limit
   after subtracting the two fixed compact integrals. The two new component
   values sum to the old value.
2. **Insertion into the first component \([k,c_1)\).** Let

   \[
    B=\lim_{x\uparrow c_1}\int_k^x f
   \]

   be the old first-component contribution, and choose an explicit regular
   anchor \(v\) with \(d<v<c_1\). Equation IMP.2a with \(u=k\) gives the new
   first contribution as the individual limit

   \[
    \int_k^x f\longrightarrow\int_k^d f\qquad(x\uparrow d).
   \]

   Equation IMP.2b gives the new bounded component's individual left-at-\(d\)
   value

   \[
    \int_x^v f\longrightarrow\int_d^v f\qquad(x\downarrow d).
   \]

   At its other endpoint, additivity gives the separate right-at-\(c_1\)
   limit

   \[
    \int_v^x f=\int_k^x f-\int_k^v f
      \longrightarrow B-\int_k^v f\qquad(x\uparrow c_1).
   \]

   The new bounded component therefore contributes

   \[
    \int_d^v f+\left(B-\int_k^v f\right)
      =B-\int_k^d f,
   \]

   since \(\int_k^v f=\int_k^d f+\int_d^v f\). Adding the new first
   contribution \(\int_k^d f\) recovers exactly \(B\). Every old limit at
   \(c_1,\ldots,c_m\) and infinity is unchanged.
3. **Insertion into a bounded interior component \((c_i,c_{i+1})\).**
   IMP.2a and IMP.2b provide the new right-of-\(c_i\)-to-\(d\) and
   \(d\)-to-left-of-\(c_{i+1}\) endpoint limits separately. Additivity, or
   equivalently anchor independence IMP-I01, shows that their four finite
   pieces have exactly the old component sum. All outer exceptional limits are
   unchanged.
4. **Insertion into the final component \((c_m,\infty)\).** IMP.2a and IMP.2b
   give both new limits at \(d\). The original \(R\to\infty\) limit, after
   subtracting the fixed integral from the old anchor to any \(v>d\), is the
   new final outer limit. The new bounded and unbounded component values sum to
   the old final contribution; every earlier exceptional limit is unchanged.

In all four cases, regular-piece `IntervalIntegrable` evidence is inherited by
restriction, every new one-sided limit exists individually, every old outer
limit is preserved, and the finite component sum is unchanged. Deleting a
marked point known to be regular reverses the same equalities. This is an
internal semantic lemma used inside T03, not a seventh public target.

## IMP-001-T01 - finite-exceptional right-ray relation

T01 is the predicate defined by IMP.1 and the preceding endpoint conditions.
Its complete contract includes all of the following rather than merely the
displayed sum:

1. \(S\subset(k,\infty)\);
2. `IntervalIntegrable f` on every compact subinterval of each component of
   \([k,\infty)\setminus S\);
3. separate one-sided limits at both sides of every \(c\in S\);
4. a one-sided limit at \(+\infty\);
5. the finite sum of component contributions equals the proposed value \(I\).

Because all five clauses are propositions and the value is an explicit
argument, T01 neither totalizes a divergent integral nor chooses a value.
IMP-I01 proves that auxiliary anchors are presentation details. This completes
the definition target.

## IMP-001-T02 - uniqueness

Assume both `HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept f k S I` and
`HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept f k S J`. Use the unique increasing enumeration
of \(S\) in both witnesses. On each component, the two witnesses give limits of
the same finite-interval function along the same nontrivial one-sided filter.
By `IMP-M03`, the first contribution is equal in the two witnesses, each left
contribution is equal, each right contribution is equal, and the final
infinite-endpoint contribution is equal. The finite sums in IMP.1 are therefore
equal, so \(I=J\).

This proof uses neither integrability across an exceptional point nor a
principal-value cancellation. The relation is a partial-value relation with at
most one value, but T02 does not define a choice-selected partial-value
function.

## IMP-001-T03 - finite-union refinement and continuous-linear combination

Let \(A\) be a finite index set. For every \(i\in A\), let
\(f_i:\mathbb R\to E\), \(I_i\in E\), a finite
\(S_i\subset(k,\infty)\), and a continuous real-linear map
\(T_i:E\to E\) be given. Put

\[
 S_A=\bigcup_{i\in A}S_i,
\]

which is again a finite subset of \((k,\infty)\), and assume

\[
 \operatorname{HasImp}(f_i,k,S_i,I_i)\qquad(i\in A).    \tag{IMP.3}
\]

The target conclusion is

\[
 \operatorname{HasImp}
 \left(t\mapsto\sum_{i\in A}T_i(f_i(t)), k, S_A,
       \sum_{i\in A}T_i(I_i)\right).                    \tag{IMP.4}
\]

First fix \(i\in A\). Enumerate the finite set
\(S_A\setminus S_i=\{d_1,\ldots,d_r\}\). Every \(d_j\) lies inside a
regular component for \(f_i\). The regular-truncation clause of IMP.3 makes
\(f_i\) `IntervalIntegrable` through that point. Induct on \(j=0,\ldots,r\):
at stage \(j\), apply all four-case insertion result IMP-I02 to \(d_{j+1}\).
The point remains regular because it is not in \(S_i\) and the previously
inserted points only subdivide its original regular component. The integral
value and every pre-existing outer limit are preserved at each finite step.
After exactly \(r\) steps we obtain

\[
 \operatorname{HasImp}(f_i,k,S_A,I_i)\qquad(i\in A).    \tag{IMP.3a}
\]

This is the refinement step; it uses no assumption that \(f_i\) is integrable
through a point of its own \(S_i\).

Now all summands have the common exceptional set \(S_A\). On any compact
\(S_A\)-regular interval, each \(f_i\) is `IntervalIntegrable` by IMP.3a. A
continuous linear map preserves Bochner integrability, and a finite sum of
integrable functions is integrable. By `IMP-M02` and finite linearity,

\[
 \int_a^b\sum_{i\in A}T_i(f_i(t))\,dt
 =\sum_{i\in A}T_i\left(\int_a^b f_i(t)\,dt\right).     \tag{IMP.5}
\]

At each left, right, or infinite endpoint of \(S_A\), apply continuity of each
\(T_i\) to the corresponding refined limit in IMP.3a, then apply finite-sum continuity
`IMP-M04`. Thus every component contribution for the integrand in IMP.4 is the
same finite continuous-linear combination of the original component
contributions. Interchanging the two finite sums is exact. Applying IMP.1 for
each \(i\) yields the proposed value in IMP.4.

This single target deliberately subsumes the only finite-linearity operations
needed downstream. Taking \(A=\varnothing\) gives zero. If one integrand has
exceptional set \(S\) and a smooth model term has the empty exceptional set,
the result is correctly stated on \(S\); no consumer must separately invoke a
regular-point insertion lemma. Identity maps on a
two-element set give addition; the map \(v\mapsto-v\) gives negation; scalar
multiplication and, for \(E=\mathbb C\), multiplication by a fixed complex
coefficient are continuous real-linear maps. No infinite sum or limit-sum
interchange is asserted.

## IMP-001-T04 - split or prepend a regular interval

Fix \(a\le k\), \(S\subset(k,\infty)\), and assume \(f\) is
`IntervalIntegrable` on \([a,k]\). Put

\[
 J=\int_a^k f(t)\,dt.
\]

The target is the equivalence

\[
 \operatorname{HasImp}(f,a,S,J+I)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{HasImp}(f,k,S,I).                        \tag{IMP.6}
\]

If \(S=\varnothing\), every \(R\ge k\) satisfies, by `IMP-M01`,

\[
 \int_a^R f=J+\int_k^R f.                               \tag{IMP.7}
\]

Taking the limit at infinity proves the forward or reverse implication by
subtracting the fixed vector \(J\).

If \(S\ne\varnothing\), only the first component changes. For every
\(b<c_1\) sufficiently near the first exceptional point,

\[
 \int_a^b f=J+\int_k^b f.                               \tag{IMP.8}
\]

All later left/right and infinite-endpoint contributions are literally the
same. Taking the left limit in IMP.8 shifts the first component contribution
by \(J\), so IMP.1 shifts the total by exactly \(J\). Conversely, subtracting
\(J\) recovers the relation from \(k\). The integrability clauses on regular
truncations are equivalent because \([a,k]\) is integrable and adjacent
intervals add. This proves IMP.6 with the displayed order \(J+I\); no sign or
orientation convention is hidden.

## IMP-001-T05 - normalized finite-exceptional primitive

For \(g:\mathbb R\to E\), \(k\in\mathbb R\), finite
\(S\subset(k,\infty)\), and explicit \(F:\mathbb R\to E\), define
`IsFiniteExceptionalPrimitive g k S F` to mean:

1. \(F\) is continuous on \([k,\infty)\);
2. \(F(k)=0\);
3. whenever \(k\le a\le b\) and \([a,b]\cap S=\varnothing\), \(g\) is
   `IntervalIntegrable` on \([a,b]\) and

   \[
     F(b)-F(a)=\int_a^b g(v)\,dv.                        \tag{IMP.9}
   \]

This definition does not include a bound on \(F\), convergence at infinity,
or a Laplace parameter. Those are separate flat hypotheses of QL-001.
Normalization removes the additive-constant ambiguity and is convenient for
telescoping from \(k\), but it does not select \(F\): the function is an
explicit argument.

Continuity has a precise semantic purpose. Fix \(c\in S\), choose regular
anchors \(d<c<e\) in its adjacent components, and apply IMP.9:

\[
 \int_d^b g=F(b)-F(d)\longrightarrow F(c)-F(d)
 \quad(b\uparrow c),                                    \tag{IMP.10}
\]

\[
 \int_a^e g=F(e)-F(a)\longrightarrow F(e)-F(c)
 \quad(a\downarrow c).                                  \tag{IMP.11}
\]

Thus the certificate supplies two independent finite values at each finite
exceptional point even when \(g\) is not norm-integrable across that point.
It does not supply a limit at infinity unless an additional hypothesis does
so. This verifies that T05 supports the intended finite-exceptional semantics
without laundering QL-001's tail conclusion into a structure field.

## IMP-001-T06 - public finite-exception exponential composition

Let `IsFiniteExceptionalPrimitive g k S F` hold, fix arbitrary
\(h\in\mathbb R\), and put \(w(t)=e^{-ht}\). Assume the two explicit
weighted-tail propositions

\[
 t\longmapsto w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(t)
 \quad\hbox{is Bochner integrable on }(k,\infty),       \tag{IMP.12a}
\]

\[
 w(R)\mathbin{\bullet}F(R)\longrightarrow0
 \quad(R\to+\infty).                                   \tag{IMP.12b}
\]

The public target is

\[
 \operatorname{HasImp}
 \left(t\mapsto w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}g(t),\ k,\ S,\
   h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_{(k,\infty)}
     w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(t)\,dt\right).             \tag{IMP.12c}
\]

The set integral in IMP.12c is honest because IMP.12a is a premise. T06 has
no positivity premise on \(h\), bound on \(F\), or quantitative conclusion.
In QL-001, \(h>0\), \(L\ge0\), and
\(\|F(t)\|\le L\) are separate flat hypotheses used to derive IMP.12a and
IMP.12b; they do not become fields of T05. The conclusion exposes both the
target relation and its exact value, so a consumer need not eliminate or
unfold T01's private representation.

### Private finite-piece Abel lemma

Let \(a\le b\), let \(g:\mathbb R\to E\) be `IntervalIntegrable` on
\([a,b]\), and let \(F:\mathbb R\to E\) be continuous on \([a,b]\). Assume

\[
 F(t)-F(a)=\int_a^t g(v)\,dv
 \qquad(a\le t\le b).                                   \tag{IMP.12d}
\]

For the same arbitrary \(h\), the internal identity is

\[
 \int_a^b w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}g(t)\,dt
 =w(b)\mathbin{\bullet}F(b)-w(a)\mathbin{\bullet}F(a)
   +h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_a^b w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(t)\,dt. \tag{IMP.13}
\]

This is the revision-3 T06 mathematics, retained byte-for-byte in substance
but no longer a public target. Its proof is the analytic engine for IMP.12c.

### `IMP-001-I03` - finite triangular Fubini identity

Set \(G(t)=\int_a^t g(v)\,dv\). On the square \([a,b]^2\), consider

\[
 H(t,v)=\bigl(\mathbf 1_{\{a\le v\le t\le b\}}w(t)\bigr)
        \mathbin{\bullet}g(v).
\]

The exponential is bounded on the compact interval: for example
\(|w(t)|\le M:=e^{|h|\max(|a|,|b|)}\). Therefore

\[
 \|H(t,v)\|\le
 \mathbf 1_{[a,b]^2}(t,v)\,M\|g(v)\|.                \tag{IMP.14}
\]

Put \(\nu=\mathrm{volume}\mathbin{\restriction}\operatorname{uIoc}(a,b)\).
The exact premise required by `MeasureTheory.intervalIntegral_integral_swap`,
with its inner measure specialized to \(\nu\), is

\[
 \operatorname{Integrable}(\operatorname{uncurry}H)
   \bigl((\mathrm{volume}\mathbin{\restriction}
          \operatorname{uIoc}(a,b))\mathbin{\mathrm{.prod}}\nu\bigr). \tag{IMP.14a}
\]

In Lean-shaped notation, the exact proposition is
`MeasureTheory.Integrable (Function.uncurry H)
((volume.restrict (Set.uIoc a b)).prod (volume.restrict (Set.uIoc a b)))`.

This is not abbreviated to separate sectionwise integrability. To prove
IMP.14a, `IntervalIntegrable g volume a b` gives integrability of
\(\lVert g\rVert\) with respect to \(\nu\), while \(\nu\) is a finite measure
with total mass \(b-a\). The norm bound IMP.14 therefore has finite integral
over the restricted product measure. The required measurability obligation is
precisely that `Function.uncurry H` is `AEStronglyMeasurable` with respect to
\(\nu\mathbin{\mathrm{.prod}}\nu\), not that it is globally
`StronglyMeasurable`. It follows measure-locally from the
`AEStronglyMeasurable` part of the integrability of \(g\) with respect to
\(\nu\), continuity of \(w\), measurability of the triangle, and continuity of
scalar multiplication. Thus no global measurability hypothesis is added. The
Bochner domination criterion proves exactly IMP.14a. Bochner Fubini `IMP-M06`
now applies; the indicator sections and the null endpoints convert its inner
restricted set integrals back to the displayed interval integrals, yielding

\[
 \int_a^b w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}G(t)\,dt
 =\int_a^b\left(\int_v^b w(t)\,dt\right)
       \mathbin{\bullet}g(v)\,dv.                       \tag{IMP.15}
\]

No infinite domain, conditional integral, or derivative of \(F\) occurs in
this interchange.

### `IMP-001-I04` - scalar exponential identity

Pinned `Real.hasDerivAt_exp` (`IMP-M10`) composed with the derivative of
\(t\mapsto-h t\) gives

\[
 \frac{d}{dt}e^{-ht}=-h e^{-ht}.
\]

The exponential and its displayed derivative are continuous, hence interval
integrable, on \([u,v]\). Apply
`intervalIntegral.integral_eq_sub_of_hasDerivAt_of_le` (`IMP-M07`) to this
derivative and multiply the result by \(-1\). This gives, without division by
\(h\),

\[
 h\int_u^v w(t)\,dt=w(u)-w(v)                           \tag{IMP.16}
\]

for every \(u\le v\). This remains true at \(h=0\), when both sides are zero.
Multiply IMP.15 by \(h\), use IMP.16, and use Bochner linearity:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_a^b w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}G(t)\,dt
 &=\int_a^b (w(v)-w(b))\mathbin{\bullet}g(v)\,dv\\
 &=\int_a^b w(v)\mathbin{\bullet}g(v)\,dv
   -w(b)\mathbin{\bullet}\int_a^b g(v)\,dv.             \tag{IMP.17}
 \end{aligned}
\]

By IMP.12d at \(t=b\), the last unweighted integral is \(F(b)-F(a)\).

### `IMP-001-I05` - constant part and final rearrangement

IMP.12d says \(F(t)=F(a)+G(t)\) on \([a,b]\). Since \(F\) and \(w\) are
continuous, the weighted primitive is `IntervalIntegrable`. Split its integral
into the constant and \(G\) parts. A second application of IMP.16 gives

\[
 h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_a^b w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(a)\,dt
 =(w(a)-w(b))\mathbin{\bullet}F(a).                     \tag{IMP.18}
\]

Adding IMP.17 and IMP.18 yields

\[
 h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_a^b w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(t)\,dt
 =\int_a^b w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}g(t)\,dt
  -w(b)\mathbin{\bullet}F(b)+w(a)\mathbin{\bullet}F(a). \tag{IMP.19}
\]

Rearranging IMP.19 is exactly IMP.13. This proves the private finite-piece
lemma. At no point was
\(F'(t)=g(t)\) assumed or derived pointwise; the only primitive information was
the finite increment law IMP.12d.

### `IMP-001-I06` - separate finite-exception endpoint limits

If a finite prefix
\([k,R]\) meets \(S\), its conditionally improper integral is **not** the
single Mathlib expression `intervalIntegral g k R`: that expression would be
totalized if \(g\) were not Bochner integrable across an exceptional point.
For every compact interval inside one regular component, T05 supplies
`IntervalIntegrable g`, the increment law IMP.12d, and continuity of \(F\).
The continuous scalar weight preserves interval integrability of \(g\), so the
private lemma applies.

At a finite exceptional point \(c\), continuity gives
\(w(t)F(t)\to w(c)F(c)\) independently from the left and right. IMP.12a makes
the weighted primitive integrable on every compact subinterval, so its
variable-endpoint interval integrals also have their individual one-sided
limits. Taking limits in IMP.13 therefore constructs all left and right
endpoint witnesses required by T01. For consecutive exceptional points
\(c_i<c_{i+1}\), the two anchor-based contributions add to

\[
 w(c_{i+1})\mathbin{\bullet}F(c_{i+1})
 -w(c_i)\mathbin{\bullet}F(c_i)
 +h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_{c_i}^{c_{i+1}}
      w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(t)\,dt.                   \tag{IMP.20}
\]

The first component has the same expression starting at \(k\); the last has
an independently truncated right endpoint. Thus this construction works for
the empty set, a singleton, and every finite \(S\), without requiring that
\(g\) be integrable across an exceptional point. All values arise from named
T05 data and the private lemma; the proof may recurse over an internal ordered
representation, but neither T06's hypotheses nor its conclusion exposes it.

### `IMP-001-I07` - telescope and pass to infinity

Add the finitely many component values only after the separate limits of
IMP-I06 have been established. Every intermediate boundary term occurs once
with each sign, and continuity identifies both copies with
\(w(c)F(c)\), so the finite exceptional terms telescope. The initial boundary
is zero because T05 includes \(F(k)=0\). For a regular right truncation \(R\),
the remaining expression is therefore

\[
 w(R)\mathbin{\bullet}F(R)
 +h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_k^R
      w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(t)\,dt.                   \tag{IMP.21}
\]

The first term tends to zero by IMP.12b. By IMP.12a and pinned `IMP-M11`, the
second tends to

\[
 h\mathbin{\bullet}\int_{(k,\infty)}
      w(t)\mathbin{\bullet}F(t)\,dt.                   \tag{IMP.22}
\]

The same argument gives the final component's separate atTop witness rather
than a coupled cutoff. Equations IMP.20--IMP.22 supply every private semantic
clause of T01 and the exact total required by IMP.12c. Hence T06 constructs the
public `HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept` relation for the original arbitrary
finite \(S\). This completes the public target without exposing, unfolding, or
requiring a consumer to reproduce the sorted recursion.

## Boundary and adversarial cases

### Empty exceptional set

When \(S=\varnothing\), T01 is precisely a right-endpoint limit of honest
`intervalIntegral` values from \(k\), with integrability on every compact
\([k,R]\). T02--T04 reduce to uniqueness, finite linearity, and additivity for
that ordinary one-sided improper integral. T06 reduces to the private
finite-piece identity followed by IMP.12a--IMP.12b at infinity; it still
constructs the direct relation without a representation-specific base-case
constructor.

### Principal-value-only singularity is rejected

Let \(c>k\), multiply \(t\mapsto1/(t-c)\) by a continuous cutoff equal to one
near \(c\), and put \(S=\{c\}\). Symmetric cutoffs around \(c\) can cancel and
produce a Cauchy principal value, but the left and right logarithmic integrals
diverge separately. T01 rejects the function. Any definition based on one
coupled pair of endpoints would incorrectly accept it and is a negative
regression.

### A genuinely conditional finite singularity is accepted

Near \(c>k\), take a cutoff times

\[
 t\longmapsto\frac{\sin(1/(t-c))}{t-c}.
\]

On either side, \(u=1/(t-c)\) converts the endpoint integral into a tail of
\(\sin u/u\), which converges by Dirichlet but not absolutely. Both one-sided
limits exist independently, so T01 accepts the example with \(S=\{c\}\).
Its accumulated primitive extends continuously through \(c\), so T05 can also
represent it. A global local-Bochner-integrability premise would wrongly reject
this intended case.

### Conditional convergence at infinity is not strengthened

For \(k>0\), \(f(t)=\sin(t)/t\) has an ordinary right-ray improper integral but
is not norm-integrable. T01 accepts it with \(S=\varnothing\). The packet never
claims that Mathlib's whole-set Bochner integral exists or equals its relational
value.

### Totalized nonintegrable finite pieces cannot fake convergence

Mathlib's integral expression is total even when an integrability proposition
is false. A predicate that mentioned only convergence of those values could
accept a meaningless constant sequence of totalized values. T01 separately
requires `IntervalIntegrable` on every regular truncation, so such a function
fails before its endpoint values are considered. The pinned negative witness
is `intervalIntegral.integral_undef` (`IMP-M08`), which makes the danger
literal: a nonintegrable finite interval integral is definitionally forced to
zero rather than left undefined.

### Degenerate finite interval and zero exponential parameter

If \(a=b\), every integral in the private identity IMP.13 is zero and the two
boundary terms cancel. If \(h=0\), IMP.13 reduces to
\(\int_a^b g=F(b)-F(a)\), exactly IMP.12d at \(b\). Public T06 also permits
\(h=0\) whenever IMP.12a says \(F\) is integrable on the ray and IMP.12b says
\(F(R)\to0\); its exact related value is then zero. No proof divides by \(h\).

### Nondifferentiable primitive

Let \(k=a<m<b\), take \(g\) to be the indicator of \([m,b]\), extended by zero
after \(b\), and set \(F(t)=\int_a^t g\). Then \(F\) is continuous, normalized,
and satisfies the T05 increment law but is not differentiable at \(m\). For any
\(h>0\), \(F\) is bounded and eventually constant, so IMP.12a and IMP.12b hold.
Public T06 applies, as does its private finite-piece lemma across \(m\). A future
proof that assumes `HasDerivAt F g` everywhere would be a strictly stronger and
rejected target.

### Continuity across an exceptional point is essential

Let \(g=0\) everywhere, let \(F=0\) on the regular component
to its left, and let \(F=1\) on the regular component to its right. The
regular-piece increment law holds on each component, but \(F\) is
discontinuous at \(c\). The improper integrals of \(g\) on both components are
zero, while the two Abel boundary terms at \(c\) differ by \(w(c)\) and do not
cancel. Thus componentwise increment laws without continuity are insufficient
for the exceptional-point composition used by QL-001. T05 explicitly rules
out this false weakening.

### Endpoint equality does not replace the increment law

On \([0,1]\), take \(g=0\) and \(F(t)=t(1-t)\). Then
\(F(1)-F(0)=\int_0^1 g=0\), so the increment identity holds at the two outer
endpoints only. For \(h>0\), however, the left side and boundary terms of
IMP.13 vanish while
\(h\int_0^1e^{-ht}F(t)\,dt>0\). Therefore the private lemma, and hence public
T06 through T05, genuinely needs the increment law for every intermediate
\(t\), not just at \(b\).

### Regular split sign

For a constant nonzero integrand on a finite prepended segment and any
convergent tail, T04 must add \(\int_a^k f\), not subtract it. Reversing \(a,k\)
without changing the interval-integral orientation is outside the target.

## Reconstruction of each target

- T01 quantifies \(E,f,k,S,I\) in that order, requires \(S\subset(k,\infty)\),
  regular-piece integrability and all independent endpoint limits, and relates
  their finite sum to \(I\).
- T02 takes two T01 witnesses for the same \(E,f,k,S\) and concludes equality
  of their explicit values.
- T03 takes finitely many T01 witnesses with one common \(k\) and individual
  finite sets \(S_i\subset(k,\infty)\), refines each witness to their literal
  finite union, and concludes T01 there for the finite continuous-real-linear
  combination with exactly the corresponding combined value.
- T04 takes \(a\le k\), a regular-piece integral \(J=\int_a^k f\), and proves
  the exact equivalence between the value \(I\) from \(k\) and \(J+I\) from
  \(a\).
- T05 takes explicit \(g,k,S,F\) and records only continuity, normalization,
  regular-piece integrability, and the increment law.
- T06 takes explicit \(g,k,S,F,h\), a T05 witness, integrability of \(wF\) on
  \((k,\infty)\), and vanishing of \(w(R)F(R)\) at infinity. For arbitrary real
  \(h\) satisfying those propositions it concludes T01, on the same literal
  \(S\), for \(wg\) at the exact value \(h\int_{(k,\infty)}wF\). The finite-piece
  identity IMP.13 is private proof infrastructure, not a seventh target.

No target has a stronger hidden premise or a smaller domain than its card.
