# OLV-001: quantitative recovery of Theorem 3.1

## Status and exact boundary

This dossier proves, in natural language, that Olver's qualitative hypotheses
produce a finite local-tail estimate at every fixed truncation order. Revision
4 changes only the dependency bindings to the prospective public revision-6
IMP interfaces and the revision-4 QL target. The mathematical target, proof,
source adapter, and architecture are unchanged from revision 3, and new
exact-commit checks for the revised dependency chain remain pending. Revision
3 preserved the source-supported complex codomain and the ordinary
finite-exceptional endpoint semantics, routed the source proof only through
public IMP and QL interfaces, and removed the source adapter's duplicated
sorted-component primitive construction. Revision 4 does not formalize the
stronger global majorant from section 9.1, freeze an exact Lean signature,
authorize Lean work, or make an approval or `lean_ready` claim.

The source transcription is
[`OLV97-C03-WATSON.md`](../source_transcriptions/OLV97-C03-WATSON.md).
That collation is awaiting independent review. A material source correction
invalidates this proof draft.

## Source hypotheses in finite-order form

Fix \(q:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C\), whose hypotheses concern positive inputs,
coefficients
\((a_s)_{s\ge0}\subset\mathbb C\), and positive real constants
\(\lambda,\mu\). The real case is obtained by specialization. Let

\[
 \alpha_s=\frac{s+\lambda-\mu}{\mu},
 \qquad \beta_s=\alpha_s+1=\frac{s+\lambda}{\mu}.
\]

For each \(n\in\mathbb N\), define

\[
 p_n(t)=\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}a_st^{\alpha_s},
 \qquad \phi_n(t)=q(t)-p_n(t).
\]

The collated source supplies:

1. for each fixed \(n\), \(\phi_n(t)=O(t^{\alpha_n})\) as
   \(t\to0^+\);
2. the improper Laplace integral of \(q\) converges over its full range for all
   sufficiently large positive \(x\);
3. a finite exceptional set \(S\subset(0,\infty)\), proper integrability on
   compact intervals avoiding \(S\), and separate ordinary one-sided Riemann
   convergence at every point of \(S\), at zero, and at infinity.

Items 2--3 are represented on the Lean-facing side by the canonical
`HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept` relation after separately proving agreement
between Olver's proper complex Riemann pieces and Mathlib's Bochner
`intervalIntegral`s. The relation's meaning is unchanged: its value is the sum
of independently convergent one-sided component values, not a principal value
or a totalized nonintegrable set integral. This Riemann-to-Bochner source
adapter remains a separate formal obligation and makes no new source claim.

For \(x>0\) and \(Q\in\mathbb C\), write

\[
 \operatorname{HasLap}(q,x,S,Q):=
 \operatorname{HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept}

Every displayed full-range Laplace integral below is shorthand for an
explicit \(Q\) satisfying OLV.R. It is never Mathlib's totalized Bochner set
integral in a conditional case. Public IMP-001-T02 uniqueness says that two
values related to the same \(q,x,S\) are equal.

## OLV-001-T01 - finite source theorem

There exists one \(X>0\) such that, for every \(n\in\mathbb N\), there exist
\(k_n>0\), \(K_n>0\), and \(L_n\ge0\) such that, for every \(x>X\), there
exists \(Q_{n,x}\in\mathbb C\) satisfying

\[
 \operatorname{HasLap}(q,x,S,Q_{n,x})                 \tag{OLV.1a}
\]

and

\[
 \left\lVert
 Q_{n,x}
 -\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}
   a_s\Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}
 \right\rVert
 \le K_n\Gamma(\beta_n)x^{-\beta_n}
   +L_ne^{-(x-X)k_n}.                                  \tag{OLV.1b}
\]

The constants are existential because arbitrary qualitative asymptotic data do
not contain computable numerical moduli. The reusable QL-001 theorem exposes
the same constants as explicit inputs; concrete applications must calculate
them. Although the witness \(Q_{n,x}\) is introduced inside the fixed-order
conclusion, uniqueness makes it independent of \(n\); the later qualitative
recovery spells out the one-function choice.

## Proof

### OLV-001-P01 - positivity of all moment exponents

For every \(s\ge0\), \(s+\lambda>0\) and \(\mu>0\), hence
\(\beta_s>0\) and \(\alpha_s>-1\). Therefore each model term is locally
integrable at zero and has a finite Gamma moment for every \(x>0\).

The value identity alone is not used as an integrability certificate. At the
pinned Mathlib revision,
`integrableOn_rpow_mul_exp_neg_mul_rpow`, specialized to power parameter
\(p=1\), exponent \(\alpha_s=\beta_s-1>-1\), and positive exponential
coefficient \(x\), proves

\[
 t\longmapsto e^{-xt}t^{\alpha_s}
 \quad\hbox{is integrable on }(0,\infty).              \tag{OLV.M1}
\]

The pinned `Real.integral_rpow_mul_exp_neg_mul_Ioi` then identifies its
honest set integral as

\[
 \Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}.                          \tag{OLV.M2}
\]

Continuous real-linear transport to \(\mathbb C\), including multiplication
by \(a_s\), preserves both integrability and this exact value.

### OLV-001-P02 - choose a common baseline convergence parameter

Before fixing \(n\), use eventual convergence to choose one positive \(X\) in
the source convergence region and an explicit value \(Q_X\) such that

\[
 \operatorname{HasLap}(q,X,S,Q_X).                    \tag{OLV.5}
\]

This same \(X\) and baseline source value are chosen before the truncation
order and used for every \(n\). Equation OLV.5 is the canonical endpoint
relation obtained from the unchanged source convergence hypothesis. Its
separate Riemann-to-Bochner adapter remains an explicit source obligation; no
component representation is unfolded here.

There is one necessary endpoint-\(0\) discharge before making that encoding.
T01 requires honest `IntervalIntegrable` evidence on compact intervals
starting at \(0\), whereas an unqualified improper Riemann limit there could
be merely conditional. Use the source expansion at the fixed order \(0\):
\(\phi_0=q=O(t^{\beta_0-1})\), with \(\beta_0>0\) by P01. Proper Riemann
regularity on compact intervals \([\varepsilon,k]\), followed by a countable
exhaustion as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), supplies the required
`AEStronglyMeasurable` fact under
\(\mathrm{volume}\mathbin{\restriction}(0,k]\). Together with the power bound,
this proves absolute local Bochner integrability at \(0\). Pointwise
`StronglyMeasurable` is neither asserted nor needed, since arbitrary
null-set modifications preserve both the source integrals and the Bochner
integral. The remaining regular pieces and separate limits at \(S\) and
infinity come from the collated ordinary-improper source hypothesis. This
conversion remains an explicit, unimplemented source-adapter obligation. The
use of fixed order \(0\) occurs before the later arbitrary \(n\); it neither
changes the quantifier order nor makes \(X\) depend on \(n\).

Now fix \(n\). For every \(s<n\), P01 proves honest `IntegrableOn` evidence
for the real model

\[
 m_{s,X}(t)=e^{-Xt}t^{\alpha_s}
\]

and its exact set-integral value

\[
 M_{s,X}=\Gamma(\beta_s)X^{-\beta_s}\in\mathbb R.      \tag{OLV.6}
\]

Apply public IMP-001-T07,
`HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept.of_integrableOn_Ioi`, with the
literal arbitrary finite set \(S\). This constructs

\[
 \operatorname{HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept}
   (m_{s,X},0,S,M_{s,X}).                              \tag{OLV.7}
\]

The `IntegrableOn` proof OLV.M1 is essential: the totalized value theorem
OLV.M2 alone is not a premise for T07.

First apply generalized public IMP-001-T03 to each OLV.7 singleton through the
continuous real-linear map
\(r\mapsto a_s(r:\mathbb C)\). This transports the real relation to a complex
relation for \(a_sm_{s,X}\) at value \(a_sM_{s,X}\). Then apply T03 again to
OLV.5 and the finitely many transported relations, using identity on the
amplitude and negation on the model terms. With

\[
 R_{n,X}=Q_X-\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}a_sM_{s,X},
\]

T03 gives exactly

\[
 \operatorname{HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept}
   (t\mapsto e^{-Xt}\phi_n(t),0,S,R_{n,X}).             \tag{OLV.8}
\]

This is finite subtraction through the public relation algebra. It neither
sorts \(S\), copies an endpoint recursion, nor identifies a conditional
improper value with a Mathlib set integral.

### OLV-001-P03 - obtain local constants

For this fixed \(n\), the source expansion at zero says

\[
 \phi_n(t)=O(t^{\alpha_n}).
\]

Unfolding one-sided big-O first gives numbers \(k_n>0\) and \(K_n\ge0\) with

\[
 \lVert\phi_n(t)\rVert\le K_nt^{\alpha_n}
 \qquad(0<t\le k_n). \tag{OLV.2}
\]

Enlarge a zero constant if necessary. If \(S\) is nonempty, also shrink
\(k_n\) below its least positive point. This gives the source-form \(K_n>0\),
preserves OLV.2, and isolates the endpoint controlled by the expansion.
Proper Riemann regularity on every
\([\varepsilon,k_n]\), \(0<\varepsilon\le k_n\), and countable exhaustion
supplies

\[
 \operatorname{AEStronglyMeasurable}\ \phi_n\
   \bigl(\mathrm{volume}\mathbin{\restriction}(0,k_n]\bigr).   \tag{OLV.2a}
\]

This measure-relative assertion is insensitive to null-set modifications and
does not claim pointwise `StronglyMeasurable`. The generic QL theorem still
permits \(K=0\).

The same domination supplies the local bridge needed before splitting.
Indeed, \(e^{-Xt}\) is bounded on \([0,k_n]\), and
\(\alpha_n>-1\); OLV.2a and OLV.2 therefore give

\[
 \operatorname{IntervalIntegrable}
   (t\mapsto e^{-Xt}\phi_n(t))\ \mathrm{volume}\ 0\ k_n.
                                                               \tag{OLV.9}
\]

This proves honest integrability at the possibly unbounded but integrable
endpoint \(0\). It is not inferred from the value of a totalized interval
integral.

### OLV-001-P04 - split, extract the primitive, and derive its bound

Put

\[
 J_{n,X}=\int_0^{k_n}e^{-Xt}\phi_n(t)\,dt,
 \qquad T_{n,X}=R_{n,X}-J_{n,X}.                       \tag{OLV.10}
\]

Because \(k_n\) was chosen below every point of \(S\), OLV.9 and public
IMP-001-T04 move the start point in OLV.8 from \(0\) to \(k_n\):

\[
 \operatorname{HasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept}
   (t\mapsto e^{-Xt}\phi_n(t),k_n,S,T_{n,X}).           \tag{OLV.11}
\]

Apply public IMP-001-T08,
`hasImproperIntegralAtTopExcept_iff_exists_primitive`,
in its forward direction. It supplies an explicit
\(F_n:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C\) such that

\[
 \operatorname{IsFiniteExceptionalPrimitive}
   (t\mapsto e^{-Xt}\phi_n(t),k_n,S,F_n),              \tag{OLV.12}
\]

\[
 F_n(t)\longrightarrow T_{n,X}\qquad(t\to+\infty).    \tag{OLV.13}
\]

Thus \(F_n(k_n)=0\), \(F_n\) is continuous on \([k_n,\infty)\), and on every
compact interval \([a,b]\) avoiding \(S\),

\[
 F_n(b)-F_n(a)=\int_a^b e^{-Xv}\phi_n(v)\,dv.           \tag{OLV.P}
\]

These are consequences of the public semantic equivalence. The source adapter
does not reconstruct \(F_n\) by sorting exceptional points and does not invoke
the private finite-piece Abel lemma.

The bound is derived after extraction; it is not a field of T08. From OLV.13,
choose \(r\ge k_n\) and \(B\ge0\) such that
\(\lVert F_n(t)\rVert\le B\) for every \(t\ge r\).
Continuity from OLV.12 makes \(F_n\) bounded by some \(C\ge0\) on the compact
interval \([k_n,r]\). Taking \(L_n=\max(B,C)\) gives

\[
 L_n\ge0,\qquad
 \lVert F_n(t)\rVert\le L_n\quad(t\ge k_n).             \tag{OLV.3}
\]

No supremum formula, computable modulus, or quantitative information is hidden
inside T08.

### OLV-001-P05 - apply the generic finite theorem

Apply revision-4 QL-001-T02 to \(\phi_n\), with

\[
 \beta=\beta_n,
 \quad k=k_n,
 \quad K=K_n,
 \quad X=X,
 \quad L=L_n,
 \quad S=S,
 \quad F=F_n.
\]

P01 supplies \(\beta_n>0\), P03 supplies the local majorant, P04 supplies the
continuous primitive certificate and its independently derived bound, and
\(x>X>0\) supplies both positive moment parameter and positive tail decay
rate. For every \(x>X\), QL-001-T02 returns an explicit \(Q_{n,x}\) together
with OLV.1a and OLV.1b. It does not return or use a totalized conditional
Bochner integral.

No infinite-series integration is used. Only the first \(n\) model terms are
integrated, and their `IntegrableOn` evidence and moments are individually
justified.

## Recovery of the printed qualitative conclusion

This section proves the mathematics needed by the future SR-001 audit theorem;
it is not itself a source-coverage declaration.

The qualitative conclusion must concern one Laplace function, not a different
choice at each truncation order. Use the \(n=0\) instance of OLV.1a to choose,
for every \(x>X\), one value \(Q_{0,x}\) related to the weighted amplitude.
Define

\[
 \mathcal L_q(x)=
 \begin{cases}
   Q_{0,x},&x>X,\\
   0,&x\le X.
 \end{cases}                                           \tag{OLV.14}
\]

This is ordinary logical choice from proved existence. It does not compute
\(\mathcal L_q\), \(k_n\), \(K_n\), or \(L_n\), and the arbitrary extension
outside the asymptotic domain carries no mathematical assertion.

For any fixed \(n\) and \(x>X\), OLV.1a supplies \(Q_{n,x}\).
Both \(Q_{n,x}\) and \(\mathcal L_q(x)\) satisfy
\(\operatorname{HasLap}(q,x,S,\cdot)\), so public IMP-001-T02 uniqueness gives

\[
 Q_{n,x}=\mathcal L_q(x).                              \tag{OLV.15}
\]

Thus every fixed-order estimate OLV.1b applies to the same function
\(\mathcal L_q\). Fix \(n\) and its witnesses. Since \(k_n>0\),

\[
 e^{-(x-X)k_n}=e^{Xk_n}e^{-k_nx}
 =o(x^{-\beta_n})\qquad(x\to+\infty).
\]

The pinned root theorem `isLittleO_exp_neg_mul_rpow_atTop` supplies the
core comparison. Hence the right side of OLV.1b is
\(O(x^{-\beta_n})\), and

\[
 \mathcal L_q(x)
 -\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}a_s\Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}
 =O(x^{-\beta_n}). \tag{OLV.4}
\]

This is Olver's Chapter 1 finite-order meaning of the expansion (3.03). Since
\(n\) was arbitrary and fixed independently of \(x\), all orders apply to the
single function OLV.14 and the entire printed asymptotic expansion follows.

## Boundary and adversarial cases

### n = 0

Then \(p_0=0\), \(\phi_0=q\), and
\(\beta_0=\lambda/\mu>0\). T03 subtracts an empty family, QL-001-T02 returns a
related value for \(q\), and OLV.1b bounds that value by a local Gamma term plus
a tail term. No coefficient access or negative finite-sum index is introduced.

### 0 < lambda < mu

The amplitude may be singular like \(t^{\lambda/\mu-1}\) at zero. P01 shows
the exponent remains greater than \(-1\), so the local integral is valid.

### Zero coefficients

Theorem 3.1 and OLV.1b permit \(a_n=0\). The proof uses the big-O remainder
constant, not a logarithmic quotient divided by \(a_n\). This differs
deliberately from the best-exponent formula in section 9.1.

### Remote bump

Adding a large bump away from zero leaves (3.02) unchanged. It changes
\(L_n\), showing why the tail witness must remain visible and why no universal
numeric constant follows from local asymptotics.

### Conditional tail and finite singularity

The T01-to-T08 extraction remains meaningful when the baseline integral is
conditionally convergent at infinity or at a finite exceptional point. T07 is
used only for the absolutely integrable Gamma models, never as a converse or
as a replacement for conditional source convergence. A totalized Bochner
integral would lose the conditional source case and may turn a divergent
integral into a misleading zero. A regression must include a cutoff multiple of
\(\sin(1/(t-c))/(t-c)\): its separate one-sided integrals converge at \(c\),
but its norm is not locally integrable there.

### Uniform families

The theorem is pointwise in all source data. It cannot be used as a uniform
parameter theorem unless common \(k,K,X,L\) are separately proved.
