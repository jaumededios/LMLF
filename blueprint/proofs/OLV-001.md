# OLV-001: quantitative recovery of Theorem 3.1

## Status and exact boundary

This dossier proves, in natural language, that Olver's qualitative hypotheses
produce a finite local-tail estimate at every fixed truncation order. It does
not formalize the stronger global majorant from section 9.1, and it does not
authorize Lean work.

The source transcription is
[`OLV97-C03-WATSON.md`](../source_transcriptions/OLV97-C03-WATSON.md).
That collation is awaiting independent review. A material source correction
invalidates this proof draft.

## Source hypotheses in finite-order form

Fix \(q\), \((a_s)_{s\ge0}\), and positive real constants
\(\lambda,\mu\). Let

\[
 \alpha_s=\frac{s+\lambda-\mu}{\mu},
 \qquad \beta_s=\alpha_s+1=\frac{s+\lambda}{\mu}.
\]

For each \(n\in\mathbb N\), define

\[
 p_n(t)=\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}a_st^{\alpha_s},
 \qquad \phi_n(t)=q(t)-p_n(t).
\]

The collated source supplies:

1. for each fixed \(n\), \(\phi_n(t)=O(t^{\alpha_n})\) as
   \(t\to0^+\);
2. the improper Laplace integral of \(q\) converges over its full range for all
   sufficiently large positive \(x\);
3. source regularity sufficient for the finite-interval integrals and
   subdivisions at the permitted finite set of discontinuities/infinities.

Item 3 must become a precise card field. It is not inferred from the two
asymptotic assertions.

## Quantifier and dependence ledger

| Data | Fixed or chosen | Permitted dependence |
|---|---|---|
| \(q,a,\lambda,\mu\) | fixed before the limit | none on \(x\) or \(n\) |
| \(X\) | chosen once in the eventual convergence region | independent of \(n\) and the later target \(x\) |
| \(n\) | arbitrary after \(X\), then fixed | proof is repeated for every \(n\) |
| \(k_n,K_n\) | chosen from local big-O | may depend on all fixed source data and \(n\) |
| \(L_n\) | chosen from bounded baseline primitive | may depend on \(n,k_n,X\) and source data |
| \(x\) | theorem input after witnesses | must exceed \(X>0\) |

The target claims no uniformity in \(n\) or other parameters.

## OLV-001-T01 - finite source theorem

There exists one \(X>0\) such that, for every \(n\in\mathbb N\), there exist
\(k_n>0\), \(K_n\ge0\), and \(L_n\ge0\) such that, for every \(x>X\), the
source Laplace integral exists and

\[
 \left\lVert
 \int_0^\infty e^{-xt}q(t)\,dt
 -\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}
   a_s\Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}
 \right\rVert
 \le K_n\Gamma(\beta_n)x^{-\beta_n}
   +L_ne^{-(x-X)k_n}. \tag{OLV.1}
\]

The constants are existential because arbitrary qualitative asymptotic data do
not contain computable numerical moduli. The reusable QL-001 theorem exposes
the same constants as explicit inputs; concrete applications must calculate
them.

## Proof

### OLV-001-P01 - positivity of all moment exponents

For every \(s\ge0\), \(s+\lambda>0\) and \(\mu>0\), hence
\(\beta_s>0\) and \(\alpha_s>-1\). Therefore each model term is locally
integrable at zero and has a finite Gamma moment for every \(x>0\).

### OLV-001-P02 - obtain local constants

Fix \(n\). The source expansion at zero says

\[
 \phi_n(t)=O(t^{\alpha_n}).
\]

Unfolding one-sided big-O gives numbers \(k_n>0\) and \(K_n\ge0\) with

\[
 \lVert\phi_n(t)\rVert\le K_nt^{\alpha_n}
 \qquad(0<t\le k_n). \tag{OLV.2}
\]

If the source model includes finitely many positive interior singularities,
shrink \(k_n\) below the first one. This preserves OLV.2 and isolates the
endpoint controlled by the expansion.

### OLV-001-P03 - choose a common baseline convergence parameter

Before fixing \(n\), use eventual convergence to choose one positive \(X\) in
the source convergence region. This same \(X\) will be used at every
truncation order.

The improper integral of every finite model term
\(e^{-Xt}t^{\alpha_s}\) is absolutely convergent by P01. Finite linearity of
improper integrals therefore shows that

\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-Xt}\phi_n(t)\,dt
\]

converges: it is the convergent integral of \(q\) minus a finite sum of
convergent model moments.

### OLV-001-P04 - obtain the bounded primitive constant

Define

\[
 F_n(t)=\int_{k_n}^{t}e^{-Xv}\phi_n(v)\,dv.
\]

The convergence established in P03 means that \(F_n(t)\) has a finite limit as
\(t\to\infty\). Every convergent function is eventually bounded. Continuity or
boundedness on each compact initial interval \([k_n,T]\), supplied by the
finite-interval integration interface, extends this to a global bound:

\[
 \lVert F_n(t)\rVert\le L_n\qquad(t\ge k_n) \tag{OLV.3}
\]

for some \(L_n\ge0\).

This step must not define \(L_n\) through an unreviewed supremum over a
possibly nonmeasurable expression. It is enough to use the elementary
existence of a bound from convergence plus compact boundedness.

### OLV-001-P05 - apply the generic finite theorem

Apply QL-001-T02 to \(\phi_n\), with

\[
 \beta=\beta_n,
 \quad k=k_n,
 \quad K=K_n,
 \quad X=X,
 \quad L=L_n.
\]

P01 supplies \(\beta_n>0\), P02 supplies the local majorant, P04 supplies the
primitive bound, and \(x>X>0\) supplies both positive moment parameter and
positive tail decay rate. The conclusion is exactly OLV.1.

No infinite-series integration is used. Only the first \(n\) model terms are
integrated, and their moments are individually justified.

## Recovery of the printed qualitative conclusion

This section proves the mathematics needed by the future SR-001 audit theorem;
it is not itself a source-coverage declaration.

Fix \(n\) and the witnesses from OLV.1. Since \(k_n>0\),

\[
 e^{-(x-X)k_n}=e^{Xk_n}e^{-k_nx}
 =o(x^{-\beta_n})\qquad(x\to+\infty).
\]

The pinned theorem `Real.isLittleO_exp_neg_mul_rpow_atTop` supplies the core
comparison. Hence the right side of OLV.1 is
\(O(x^{-\beta_n})\), and

\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-xt}q(t)\,dt
 -\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}a_s\Gamma(\beta_s)x^{-\beta_s}
 =O(x^{-\beta_n}). \tag{OLV.4}
\]

This is Olver's Chapter 1 finite-order meaning of the expansion (3.03). Since
\(n\) was arbitrary and fixed independently of \(x\), the entire printed
asymptotic expansion follows.

## Boundary and adversarial cases

### n = 0

Then \(p_0=0\), \(\phi_0=q\), and
\(\beta_0=\lambda/\mu>0\). The theorem bounds the entire integral by a local
Gamma term plus a tail term. No negative finite-sum index is introduced.

### 0 < lambda < mu

The amplitude may be singular like \(t^{\lambda/\mu-1}\) at zero. P01 shows
the exponent remains greater than \(-1\), so the local integral is valid.

### Zero coefficients

Theorem 3.1 and OLV.1 permit \(a_n=0\). The proof uses the big-O remainder
constant, not a logarithmic quotient divided by \(a_n\). This differs
deliberately from the best-exponent formula in section 9.1.

### Remote bump

Adding a large bump away from zero leaves (3.02) unchanged. It changes
\(L_n\), showing why the tail witness must remain visible and why no universal
numeric constant follows from local asymptotics.

### Conditional tail

The bounded-primitive proof remains meaningful when the baseline integral is
conditionally convergent. Replacing it with a totalized Bochner integral would
lose this source case and may turn a divergent integral into a misleading zero.

### Uniform families

The theorem is pointwise in all source data. It cannot be used as a uniform
parameter theorem unless common \(k,K,X,L\) are separately proved.

## Relationship to section 9

The separately transcribed equation (9.02) assumes

\[
 \lVert\phi_n(t)\rVert
 \le |a_n|t^{\beta_n-1}e^{\sigma_nt}
 \quad\text{for every }t>0.
\]

This immediately gives a one-term Gamma majorant with
\((x-\sigma_n)^{-\beta_n}\), but that global assumption is not supplied by
Theorem 3.1. It should become a separate direct-source target after this packet,
not an internal shortcut.
