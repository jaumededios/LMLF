# OLV-002: supplied global majorant implies the Watson bound

## Status and exact boundary

This is a complete first natural-language proof draft, but it is unreviewed,
unfrozen, planning-only, and authorizes no Lean work. Its sole mathematical
target is Olver (1997), Chapter 3, section 9.1, equation (9.01) implying
equation (9.02). The packet assumes the displayed global majorant as data. It
does not construct or optimize its exponential rate, relax the leading
constant, or develop later refinements.

The source binding is the first, independently unreconciled transcription
[`OLV97-C03-WATSON-BOUNDS.md`](../source_transcriptions/OLV97-C03-WATSON-BOUNDS.md).
The exact scan was visually checked at printed page 89, PDF page 102. A material
source correction invalidates this draft.

## Scalar choice and source relationship

The conservative reading of the chapter title and surrounding discussion is
real-valued. This draft nevertheless writes the proof for
\(\mathbb K=\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\), using absolute value in the real case
and norm in the complex case. The complex statement is explicitly a project
generalization, not a claim about what Olver intended. Specializing
\(\mathbb K\) to \(\mathbb R\) recovers the printed implication without a
constant or domain change.

The open structural choice before freezing is whether to expose:

1. a real direct-source target and a later complex corollary; or
2. one scalar-polymorphic target whose real specialization is identified as
   the source result.

This choice changes packaging and novelty labeling, but not the proof below.

## Source reconciliation

Fix a truncation order \(n\in\mathbb N\), positive real parameters
\(\lambda,\mu\), and set

\[
 \beta_n=\frac{n+\lambda}{\mu}.
\]

Olver's \(\phi_n\) is the remainder already defined in equation (3.04), and
\(a_n\) is its first neglected coefficient. The source then supplies the
global hypothesis

\[
 \lVert\phi_n(t)\rVert
 \le \lVert a_n\rVert t^{\beta_n-1}e^{\sigma_nt}
 \qquad(0<t<\infty). \tag{OLV-002.1}
\]

For the real specialization, the norms in OLV-002.1 are exactly the absolute
values printed in (9.01). The source identifies the error term in (3.05) with
the integral of \(e^{-xt}\phi_n(t)\), and (9.02) is precisely the conclusion
stated below. The scan's footnote records that \(x>0\) is needed for (3.05);
the printed domain \(x>\max(\sigma_n,0)\) retains that condition.

The abstract proof uses no other property of the equation-(3.04) definition.
Treating \(\phi_n\) and \(a_n\) as fixed inputs is therefore a transparent
reformulation of this conditional implication, not a derivation of OLV-002.1
from the qualitative Watson hypotheses.

## Integral and endpoint convention

The target uses the Lebesgue/Bochner integral on \((0,\infty)\). Strong
measurability is stated explicitly because domination by an integrable
function does not by itself make a nonmeasurable function integrable. Once the
comparison in the proof is established, it proves absolute integrability at
both endpoints, so no conditionally convergent or totalized improper integral
is involved.

The target takes an ordinary \(\mathbb K\)-valued remainder. Read literally,
the finite pointwise majorant for every \(t>0\) rules out infinite values of
\(\phi_n\); the source's broader permission for isolated infinities therefore
does not create an additional case inside this implication.

## Exact identities

### OLV-002-I01 - exponent normalization

By definition,

\[
 \frac{n+\lambda-\mu}{\mu}
 =\frac{n+\lambda}{\mu}-1=\beta_n-1.
\]

Thus OLV-002.1 has exactly the power printed in (9.01).

### OLV-002-I02 - positive decay gap

Set \(\delta=x-\sigma_n\). Since
\(x>\max(\sigma_n,0)\), one has both \(x>0\) and
\(\delta>0\).

### OLV-002-I03 - exponential combination

For every \(t>0\),

\[
 e^{-xt}e^{\sigma_nt}=e^{-(x-\sigma_n)t}=e^{-\delta t}.
\]

All exponential factors here are positive real scalars, so multiplication by
them scales either the real absolute value or the complex norm exactly.

### OLV-002-I04 - Gamma moment at the decay gap

Because \(n\ge0\), \(\lambda>0\), and \(\mu>0\),
\(\beta_n>0\). With \(\delta>0\), OLV2-E02 gives the moment directly;
OLV2-E05 rewrites its positive-base power expression into the source form

\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\delta t}t^{\beta_n-1}\,dt
 =\Gamma(\beta_n)\delta^{-\beta_n}
 =\frac{\Gamma(\beta_n)}{(x-\sigma_n)^{\beta_n}}. \tag{OLV-002.2}
\]

Every power has positive real base, so no complex branch convention occurs.

## OLV-002-T01 - supplied-majorant bound

### Statement

Under D01-D08, for every \(x>\max(\sigma_n,0)\), the integral is absolutely
convergent and

\[
 \left\lVert\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}\phi_n(t)\,dt\right\rVert
 \le
 \Gamma(\beta_n)
 \frac{\lVert a_n\rVert}{(x-\sigma_n)^{\beta_n}}. \tag{OLV-002.3}
\]

For \(\mathbb K=\mathbb R\), this is (9.02) from the supplied hypothesis
(9.01). The complex instance is the declared generalization.

### Proof

#### OLV-002-P01 - pointwise domination

Fix an arbitrary \(x>\max(\sigma_n,0)\) and let
\(\delta=x-\sigma_n\). For each \(t>0\), positivity of \(e^{-xt}\), the
supplied majorant, and I03 give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert e^{-xt}\phi_n(t)\rVert
 &=e^{-xt}\lVert\phi_n(t)\rVert\\
 &\le e^{-xt}\lVert a_n\rVert
       t^{\beta_n-1}e^{\sigma_nt}\\
 &=\lVert a_n\rVert t^{\beta_n-1}e^{-\delta t}.
\end{aligned} \tag{OLV-002.4}
\]

This is L01. It is valid in both proposed scalar codomains.

#### OLV-002-P02 - absolute integrability

By I04, the nonnegative right-hand side of OLV-002.4 has finite integral

\[
 \lVert a_n\rVert
 \frac{\Gamma(\beta_n)}{(x-\sigma_n)^{\beta_n}}.
\]

The target integrand is strongly measurable by D06 and multiplication by the
continuous scalar exponential. Measurable domination E04 therefore proves
absolute integrability of \(e^{-xt}\phi_n(t)\) on \((0,\infty)\). This also
establishes existence of the displayed integral; existence is not smuggled in
through a totalized integral notation.

#### OLV-002-P03 - integrate the inequality

Apply E03, then integrate OLV-002.4, and finally apply I04:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left\lVert\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}\phi_n(t)\,dt\right\rVert
 &\le \int_0^\infty
       \lVert e^{-xt}\phi_n(t)\rVert\,dt\\
 &\le \lVert a_n\rVert
       \int_0^\infty e^{-\delta t}t^{\beta_n-1}\,dt\\
 &=\Gamma(\beta_n)
   \frac{\lVert a_n\rVert}{(x-\sigma_n)^{\beta_n}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is OLV-002.3. Since \(x\) was arbitrary in exactly the printed strict
domain, the proof reconstructs the target quantifier order without choosing a
new rate or shrinking the domain.

## Boundary and adversarial cases

### Order zero

At \(n=0\), \(\beta_0=\lambda/\mu>0\). Nothing in the proof uses a predecessor
coefficient or a nonempty retained sum, so the same implication applies.

### Vanishing coefficient

If \(a_n=0\), OLV-002.1 forces \(\phi_n(t)=0\) for every \(t>0\). Both sides of
OLV-002.3 are zero. The proof never divides by \(a_n\), so this case needs no
exception.

### Singular power at zero

When \(0<\beta_n<1\), the factor \(t^{\beta_n-1}\) diverges at zero but is
integrable there. Positivity of \(\beta_n\), rather than nonnegativity of the
power exponent, is the exact required condition.

### Negative supplied rate

If \(\sigma_n<0\), the target still retains the source condition \(x>0\), even
though \(x-\sigma_n>0\) would allow some negative \(x\). This is intentional:
the packet reproduces the printed domain and the source error-term context.

### Boundary value of x

The nearby statement with \(x=\sigma_n\ge0\) is false in general. For example,
take nonzero \(a_n\) and
\(\phi_n(t)=a_nt^{\beta_n-1}e^{\sigma_nt}\). The majorant is an equality, while
the target integrand at \(x=\sigma_n\) is
\(a_nt^{\beta_n-1}\), whose tail integral diverges. Thus the strict inequality
and the denominator margin cannot be weakened to equality.

### Complex phase

No ordering of complex values is used. Only the norm majorant, positive real
scalar multiplication, and the Bochner integral triangle inequality occur, so
arbitrary complex phase does not change the constant.

### Measurability is not cosmetic

A pointwise bound by an integrable function does not make an arbitrary
nonmeasurable function Lebesgue integrable. Hence D06 must remain visible unless
the future integral interface packages measurability into the definition of the
source remainder.

## Trust, imports, and exclusions

The future implementation should need only narrow measure-integration,
real-exponential, real-power, norm, and Gamma-moment support. Exact imports and
the public signature are intentionally deferred; there is no prototype. The
argument uses ordinary classical real analysis and no numerical certificate,
floating-point evaluation, interval arithmetic, quadrature, tolerance choice,
adaptive truncation, or external solver.

No formula beyond the supplied-majorant implication belongs to this artifact.
In particular, it exposes no mechanism for manufacturing a rate from
\(\phi_n\), and it makes no claim that the premise follows from the local
asymptotic expansion alone.
